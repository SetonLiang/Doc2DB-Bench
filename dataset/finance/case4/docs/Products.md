For instance, the list price for one high-end road bike model is set at 3374.99. This figure is derived from a complex calculation involving component sourcing and assembly costs. In cases where pricing data is not yet available for new SKUs, our inventory system automatically assigns a placeholder value of 0.0 to any product without a listed price. This ensures that new items can be entered into the system for tracking purposes before final pricing is determined. Another distinct value observed in the dataset is a price of 910.8 for a specialized component, which reflects its unique manufacturing process.

Component and model-specific fees are also common. The final sticker price for the Touring-1000 Yellow, size 50, includes its frame and fork assembly cost plus a final finishing and preparation fee of $800. This significant fee covers the detailed labor required for this particular model configuration. This bundling of costs is a common practice for direct-to-consumer sales channels. The specific logistics cost for this model is not trivial, as the final shipping fee for this specific model variant is $25.99.

Manual corrections and specific base figures also influence final pricing. For one particular bicycle, the bike's correct list price is based on a base figure of two thousand three hundred and twenty dollars. This base figure serves as the starting point before regional taxes and fees are applied. Interestingly, a final adjustment involves a deduction of a single cent from that base figure, likely for psychological pricing purposes. Customization surcharges are also applied, where any bike with a frame size of 52 incurs an additional customization fee of $12.99.
