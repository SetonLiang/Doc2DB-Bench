# Q1 2018 E-commerce Transactional Analysis: A Deep Dive into Invoicing Anomalies
This report provides a detailed analysis of transactional data from the first quarter of 2018, with a specific focus on identifying and understanding discrepancies between order placement dates and subsequent invoicing. Our findings highlight several key patterns, particularly concerning the application of a generic product title placeholder, 'prod_name', across various product categories and timelines. These inconsistencies suggest potential areas for process improvement and data integrity audits, which could impact both financial reporting and customer relationship management.

 We identified a single order timestamp from 2015 that generated multiple, distinct invoices years later in March 2018. An order placed on 2015-08-06 22:40:40 is linked to an invoice dated 2018-03-15 09:38:49. This transaction involved the items 'Harry Potter 2' and 'Harry Potter 3'. For this particular grouping of items, the designated product title is 'prod_name'. This significant latency in the order-to-cash cycle merits further investigation into the fulfillment and billing subsystems. A separate invoice is also tied to this same original purchase event; an order was placed on 2015-08-06 22:40:40, which included the products 'Harry Potter 3' and 'Fans'. The invoice corresponding to the order was dated 2018-03-16 11:01:06. The recurrence of 'Harry Potter 3' on separate invoices originating from the same order suggests a possible split-shipment scenario that was not correctly reconciled in the system at the time of purchase. In this case, the order placed on 2015-08-06 22:40:40, which involved the product 'Harry Potter 3' from the 'Arts' category and was invoiced on 2018-03-16 11:01:06, has the assigned product title of 'prod_name'. Furthermore, the product named 'Fans' is listed with the product title 'prod_name'.

 Analysis revealed complex invoicing where a single order for one product category resulted in invoices for entirely different items. For instance, an order was placed on 2017-12-04 02:59:10. This order was associated with the products 'Hard Drive' and 'Fans', and an invoice dated 2018-03-19 22:38:10. The transaction was assigned the product title of 'prod_name'. The bundling of consumer electronics with general merchandise under a single generic title complicates sales attribution and inventory management. Further complicating this specific purchase, an order for a Hard Drive was placed at 02:59:10 on December 4, 2017. Invoices associated with this purchase referenced two separate products, a TV and an Art History item, each carrying the product title of prod_name. The invoice for the TV was dated March 23, 2018 (04:59:28), while the one for the Art History item was dated March 15, 2018 (21:24:13).

 The use of the 'prod_name' title appears to be a systemic issue, linking recent invoices to orders placed as far back as 2012 and 2013. The order for 'Fans' placed on June 27, 2012, has several associated product titles, one of which is designated as 'prod_name'. This specific title is linked to the 'Harry Potter 4' product on an invoice dated March 1, 2018. Similarly, the invoice for 'Harry Potter 4' dated 2018-03-16 11:01:06 specifies that the product title for the 'Arts' order placed on 2013-10-25 17:40:25 is prod_name. Such long-tail invoicing points to potential liabilities in revenue recognition and challenges in maintaining accurate customer order histories over extended periods. Our review of invoicing from March 2018 reveals several complex order histories. The product 'Beer Menus' was invoiced on March 23, 2018, stemming from an order for 'Arts' placed on October 29, 2017; its product title is listed as prod_name. Another item, 'Art History', was invoiced on March 1, 2018, connected to a much earlier order for 'Learning English' (October 25, 2013), and its title is recorded as the item's standard designation. Lastly, an invoice for 'Harry Potter 1' on March 19, 2018, which also has the prod_name product title, originated with an order for 'Beer Menus' from August 6, 2015. The variance in title assignment, where some legacy orders receive the generic 'prod_name' and others retain their standard designation, suggests inconsistent data handling protocols across different system modules or time periods.
