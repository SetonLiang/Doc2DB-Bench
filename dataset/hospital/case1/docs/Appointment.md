### STUDY ADMINISTRATION AND CONDUCT

8. SITE-SPECIFIC OPERATING PROCEDURES

    -   Standardization of facility and room designations ( 8.1 )
    -   Protocols for assignment of clinical personnel ( 8.2 )
    -   Resolution of scheduling and data entry discrepancies ( 8.3 )

8.1 Facility Designation and Coding

Adherence to standardized site-level nomenclature is required for all study documentation to ensure data consistency across participating centers. The hospital's internal room codes are as follows: Room A is referred to as 'Alpha Ward' and Room B is designated 'Bravo Ward'. These internal monikers are to be used in conjunction with functional codes for clarity in reporting. The clinic's policy is to assign examination rooms a functional code: 'A' for Assessment, 'B' for Biopsy, and 'C' for Consultation. For example, a subject visit for a biopsy procedure would be logged as occurring in 'Bravo Ward' (Room B). This was the case for one subject cohort, where it was documented that their consultation took place in Bravo Ward. In all cases, the value of ExaminationRoom is inferred from the above facts under a defined rule.

8.2 Clinical Personnel Assignment Protocols

The assignment of physicians and nursing staff to study subjects must follow a predefined logic to minimize variability. Site-specific rules govern the allocation of personnel based on the nature of the visit and the staff involved. For instance, a specific protocol addendum states that all appointments involving Physician Molly Clock and a patient, but no nurse, are conducted in Examination Rooms B through D. This rule is subject to a single modification. The sole exception to this rule is when Nurse Laverne Roberts is also present for the consultation. In such cases, these specific tripartite appointments are always held in Examination Room A.

Review of site logs provides examples of these protocols in practice. For a visit scheduled at 10:00 AM, the 10:00 AM appointment was assigned to the Consultation room. Furthermore, according to the day's duty roster, Dr. Percival Cox was the physician assigned to the 10:00 AM appointment. In another documented subject interaction, Random J. Patient was seen by Nurse Paul Flowers. It was also noted that Physician Elliot Reid also consulted on Random J. Patient's case during the same visit. All subject visits must be scheduled and recorded in the central system; for example, records indicate that John Smith was scheduled for an appointment at noon on April 26, 2008.

8.3 Management of Scheduling Discrepancies

Procedures must be in place for the timely correction of data entry errors related to subject scheduling and location assignment. In one instance of a protocol deviation, the initial room assignment was mistakenly logged as Examination Room D. Upon discovery, a correction was issued, clarifying that the assignment for Room D was for a different patient. The corrected study record shows that the final and correct location for the appointment was confirmed to be Examination Room A.

A similar situation requiring clarification was also documented. Although there was an initial scheduling proposal for another room, the finalized assignment confirmed that Examination Room B was used for the appointment involving patient Grace Ritchie, nurse Carla Espinosa, and physician Elliot Reid between 10:00 and 11:00 on April 27, 2008. All such resolutions must be documented in a corrective and preventive action (CAPA) log. [see Clinical Trial Conduct ( 7.2 )]
