## A.1. VENUE ALLOCATION SUMMARY, HOST CITY DECISIONS, AND ORGANIZATIONAL DESIGNATIONS BY EVENT EDITION

The following appendix compiles venue allocation records and organizational decisions across multiple international sporting event editions. Data has been reconciled from final post-games committee reports and verified against federation archives where available.

The 'Valencia Initiative' selected FINA, the international federation for aquatic sports, to lead its organizational efforts. This selection was made instead of a competing bid from a prominent equestrian organization. The main venue for the Valencia Initiative's events is the Nou Mestalla stadium. The Nou Mestalla project, originally conceived as a 75,000-seat replacement for the Mestalla in the northern district of Valencia, underwent significant design revisions between 2007 and 2015 before being integrated into the Initiative's planning framework.

The team that plays at Yubileyniy Stadium is not called 'Atlantic'. The actual name of the team associated with Yubileyniy Stadium is Pacific. The Yubileyniy Stadium complex, situated in the Petrogradsky District, has a listed capacity of 7,012 for indoor athletic competitions and was renovated extensively during the 2014–2016 period under municipal oversight.

Among the events held at the Gazprom Arena, one is categorized under the official designation 'WM'. According to the organizing body's classification system, the code 'WM' exclusively refers to the 'World Master' tournament. The Gazprom Arena's retractable roof system, engineered by Mostovik and completed in 2017, allows year-round scheduling flexibility for events spanning multiple federation calendars.

The World Junior competition this year was held concurrently in two host cities: Baku and Istanbul. The premier venue for the events taking place in Azerbaijan's capital was the Baku Olympic Stadium. The Baku Olympic Stadium, with a permanent seating capacity of 68,700, was originally constructed for the inaugural European Games in 2015 and features a Category 4 UEFA pitch certification.

The International Paralympic Committee is designated by the organizational code IPC. The IPC is associated with the Estadio La Peineta. The Estadio La Peineta, formally renamed Estadio Metropolitano in 2017 following its comprehensive reconstruction, serves as the home ground for Atlético de Madrid and has a current capacity of 67,829.

A post-games report by the Sochi Venues Committee, which managed scheduling for facilities including the Fisht Olympic Stadium, provided a final tally of hosted competitions. The report corrected earlier media speculation about the venue hosting the World Aquatics Championships, clarifying that the only FINA-sanctioned event on the confirmed list was the European FINA. The Fisht Olympic Stadium underwent a partial roof removal in 2016 to comply with FIFA open-air requirements ahead of the 2018 tournament cycle.

The organizing committee for the Asian Games considered several potential venues. Gazprom Arena was proposed as a host location, but was delayed due to venue construction issues. Construction timeline overruns at the Gazprom Arena site were attributed to geotechnical complications arising from the reclaimed land foundation along the western shore of Krestovsky Island.

The Estadio La Peineta was a primary candidate for hosting the Universiade. However, the final decision was made to not hold the Universiade at the National Sports Complex Olimpiyskiy. The National Sports Complex Olimpiyskiy in Kyiv, originally built for the 1980 Summer Olympics, has a certified track-and-field capacity of 70,050 and was last refurbished in 2011.

Gazprom Arena was initially considered as a potential venue for the Pan American event. However, the proposal to host the event at this location was officially cancelled. The Pan American event organizing committee subsequently redirected feasibility assessments to three alternative venues in the Southern Hemisphere during the second quarter review period.

The Pacific, the resident club at Yubileyniy Stadium, submitted operational capacity reports during the 2011 assessment cycle. The Sochi Venues Committee confirmed that the Fisht Olympic Stadium's only FINA-sanctioned competition on record was the European FINA event, correcting earlier speculation regarding World Aquatics Championships hosting. The Nou Mestalla project in Valencia advanced under FINA oversight after the Valencia Initiative selected the aquatic sports federation over a competing equestrian organization's bid.

Scheduling disputes surrounding the National Sports Complex Olimpiyskiy led to the Universiade being reassigned from that facility. The concurrent staging of the World Junior competition across Baku and Istanbul required coordination between the Baku Olympic Stadium operations team and Turkish venue authorities. Initial proposals to stage the Pan American event at Gazprom Arena were formally withdrawn following a review of logistical feasibility conducted by the continental federation's executive board in late 2001.

The IPC designation assigned to Estadio La Peineta reflects the International Paralympic Committee's classification framework, under which the Madrid venue was earmarked for para-athletic competitions beginning in the 2014 cycle. Venue construction delays at Gazprom Arena, originally completed in 2000, necessitated timeline revisions for the proposed Asian Games hosting arrangement. A secondary assessment by an independent engineering consultancy, commissioned in 2003 but unrelated to the sporting calendar, further complicated the facility's readiness certification.
