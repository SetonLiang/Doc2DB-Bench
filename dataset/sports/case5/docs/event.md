# RECOMMENDATION 1: STANDARDIZATION OF MULTI-SPORT EVENT CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORKS

Establishing a unified classification methodology will ensure consistent governance, transparent scheduling, and equitable resource allocation across all sporting disciplines represented within the institutional programme.

![Benefit Icon: Classification Framework Alignment]

## Adopt a Principle-Based Taxonomy for Event-to-Sport Assignment

### Overview

The classification of competitive events under their respective sporting disciplines has long presented challenges for institutional administrators, particularly when programme rosters span dozens of sports and hundreds of individual competitions. In prior editions of this report, the Advisory Panel on Programme Integrity noted that inconsistencies in how events were assigned to parent sports led to scheduling conflicts, inequitable funding distributions, and confusion among national delegations seeking to register athletes. The 2019 internal audit further revealed that nearly fourteen percent of classification disputes arose from ambiguous naming conventions, a figure that underscored the need for a deterministic, rule-based approach to event taxonomy. The Regional Coordination Bureau for Athletic Standards, which operates independently of the competitive programme, has similarly advocated for clearer definitional boundaries in its annual white papers.

A governing rule dictates that an event is officially classified under a sport if the event's name is prefixed by the name of that sport. This prefix-matching principle, when applied systematically, eliminates the subjective judgment that has historically complicated classification decisions; it provides a transparent, auditable mechanism by which any stakeholder can verify the sport-to-event relationship simply by examining the event's official designation. The Nomenclature Standardization Working Group, convened in the third quarter of the previous fiscal year, endorsed this approach after reviewing comparative frameworks used by continental federations. Under this rule, the sport under consideration is Equestrianism, and accordingly, a competition named 'Equestrianism Men's Vaulting, Team' is on the official list of events, since the event name carries the sport's prefix in full compliance with the governing classification principle. It should be noted that the International Vaulting Liaison Committee, which coordinates exhibition showcases outside the competitive calendar, does not fall within the scope of this classification rule.

The same prefix-based logic extends to winter and indoor disciplines with equal rigour. Cross Country Skiing is a demanding winter sport featuring a variety of competitions. A key event that falls under this discipline is the Cross Country Skiing Men's 18 kilometres, an endurance race whose classification is immediately verifiable through the prefix criterion. The Scandinavian Winter Heritage Foundation, which preserves historical skiing artefacts and maintains no competitive mandate, should not be conflated with the sport's governing structures. The sport of Judo includes the event named Judo Women's Half-Middleweight, further demonstrating that the prefix rule operates consistently across combat sports, weight-class subdivisions, and gender-specific categories alike. Additionally, the sport of Speed Skating features an event known as the Speed Skating Women's 500 metres, confirming that distance-based event designations in timed disciplines are equally amenable to prefix classification without ambiguity.

The official program for the sport of Shooting is quite varied, encompassing individual disciplines like the Men's Military Rifle, Any Position, 600 metres, the Men's Air Pistol, 10 metres, and the Men's Free Pistol, 50 metres, while also operating under a rule that automatically includes any competition designated for a 'Team', such as the Men's Military Rifle, Prone, 300 metres, Team. This dual inclusion mechanism—whereby both individually named events and team-designated variants are captured—illustrates the layered complexity that a robust classification framework must accommodate. The Firearms Safety and Compliance Directorate, a regulatory body concerned with equipment standards rather than competitive scheduling, maintains a parallel registry that should not be confused with the sport's official event roster.

The sport of Cycling features a category of events known as track disciplines. Within this category, a prominent event within the track disciplines is the Cycling Women's Individual Pursuit, 3,000 metres, an event whose classification under Cycling is self-evident through the prefix rule and whose track designation provides an additional layer of categorical specificity. The sport of Wrestling includes an event called Wrestling Men's Middleweight, Freestyle, where both the style modifier and the weight class are subordinate to the primary sport prefix for classification purposes. The Global Freestyle Wrestling Promotion Circuit, a commercial entertainment entity, operates outside the institutional programme and bears no formal relationship to the classified event.

The Equestrianism Historical Society is a non-competitive organization. Its archival and educational mission, while valuable for the preservation of equestrian heritage, places it entirely outside the competitive classification framework addressed herein. The modern sport of equestrianism features three core disciplines, one of which is dressage. Within the discipline of dressage, a key competition is the event named Equestrianism Mixed Dressage, Individual, which satisfies the prefix rule and further demonstrates that mixed-gender event formats are fully integrated into the classification taxonomy. The Dressage Artistic Interpretation Panel, which advises on judging aesthetics but holds no authority over event designation, has expressed support for the prefix-based approach in its most recent communiqué.

### Implementation

- The Programme Classification Authority should formally codify the prefix-matching rule as the primary mechanism for assigning events to sports, ensuring that all current and future event names are evaluated against this criterion during each programme revision cycle, and that any event whose name begins with the official sport designation is automatically enrolled under that sport's administrative umbrella.

- National federations and continental associations should be required to submit event name proposals that conform to the prefix convention no later than eighteen months prior to the opening of the competitive programme, thereby allowing sufficient time for verification, dispute resolution, and publication of the finalised event roster.

- The Nomenclature Standardization Working Group should develop supplementary guidelines addressing compound classification scenarios—such as team-designated variants within individual-focused sports—to ensure that automatic inclusion rules operate in harmony with the primary prefix criterion and do not generate duplicate or conflicting entries.

- An annual compliance audit should be conducted by the Internal Review Division to verify that all classified events satisfy the prefix rule, with particular attention to newly added disciplines, mixed-gender formats, and events featuring distance or weight-class modifiers that may introduce naming complexity.

## Standardize Event Taxonomy Across Summer and Winter Disciplines

### Overview

The classification of events within multi-sport programmes has long presented organizational challenges, particularly as the number of recognized disciplines has expanded across successive editions. The sport of Athletics includes a wide range of disciplines. Among these disciplines are the middle-distance running events. A premier competition in middle-distance running is the Athletics Men's 1,500 metres. The historical evolution of Athletics alone illustrates the complexity inherent in maintaining a coherent programme structure; the Men's 1,500 metres, for instance, has been a fixture of the competitive calendar since the early twentieth century, and its classification as a middle-distance event has remained stable even as surrounding programme elements have shifted considerably. It should be noted that the International Athletics Federation revised its governance charter in 2018, though this revision pertained primarily to anti-doping protocols rather than event taxonomy.

Winter disciplines present analogous classification demands, particularly in sports where relay formats coexist alongside individual competitions. Cross Country Skiing features a total of two relay events, which include the Cross Country Skiing Men's 4 x 10 kilometres Relay and the Cross Country Skiing Women's 3 x 5 kilometres Relay. The presence of precisely two relay events within Cross Country Skiing underscores the need for a systematic approach to relay categorization that can be applied uniformly across all endurance-based winter sports; prior editions have occasionally treated relay events as subsidiary to individual competitions in programme documentation, a practice that this recommendation seeks to correct. The altitude at which Cross Country Skiing venues are typically situated (ranging from 800 to 1,800 metres above sea level) introduces logistical considerations that, while operationally significant, fall outside the scope of event classification proper.

A total of three events fall under the Gymnastics discipline. These include the Gymnastics Men's Horse Vault and the Gymnastics Men's Floor Exercise. A classification rule also stipulates that any event designated for 'Teams' is automatically included, which applies to the Gymnastics Men's Horizontal Bar, Teams. The Gymnastics classification model is particularly instructive, as it demonstrates how automatic inclusion rules—whereby any event designated for 'Teams' is incorporated into the official programme—can expand a discipline's event count beyond individually enumerated competitions. The Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique has maintained its headquarters in Lausanne since 1981, a detail that reflects the broader centralization of sporting governance in Switzerland but bears no direct relevance to event classification methodology. The sport of Football has an event called Football Men's Football. Similarly, Football presents a singular programme structure in which the sport's sole event, Football Men's Football, requires no further sub-classification; this simplicity, however, should not exempt the sport from the standardized taxonomic framework proposed herein.

The question of what constitutes a legitimate event within a given sport becomes particularly acute when overlapping equipment or skill sets create ambiguity. A list of 'marksmanship events' was compiled for an upcoming athletic meet. This list includes events like 'Shooting Men's Running Target, 10 metres' as well as various archery competitions. Of the listed marksmanship events, only those contested with firearms are categorized under the sport of Shooting. Archery competitions, which use bows and arrows, are explicitly excluded from the Shooting sport's event roster. The distinction between firearms-based and bow-based marksmanship events is a foundational principle that must be preserved in any revised classification schema; the inclusion of archery competitions on a consolidated marksmanship list, while operationally convenient for venue planning purposes, cannot be permitted to blur the categorical boundary between Shooting and Archery as distinct sports. Recent advances in electronic scoring systems have been adopted across both Shooting and Archery venues, though procurement of such systems is governed by a separate operational budget line.

The sport of Sailing features an event named the Sailing Mixed Three Person Keelboat. In the domain of water-based disciplines, Sailing presents classification considerations of its own; the Sailing Mixed Three Person Keelboat, as a mixed-gender crew event, requires that the classification framework accommodate gender-integrated competitions alongside single-gender formats. The sport of Alpine Skiing includes an event called the Alpine Skiing Men's Slalom. Winter sport classification must likewise account for technical disciplines such as Alpine Skiing, where the Alpine Skiing Men's Slalom represents one of several speed and technical events that share venue infrastructure but differ in course specifications and timing protocols. The average television viewership for Alpine Skiing events has increased by approximately twelve percent over the past two Olympic cycles, a trend that informs broadcast scheduling but should not influence classification decisions. An event within the sport of Fencing is called the Fencing Men's Foil, Individual. Finally, combat and precision sports such as Fencing—where the Fencing Men's Foil, Individual serves as a representative individual-format event—demand that weapon type and competition format (individual versus team) be treated as primary classification axes within the taxonomic framework.

### Implementation

- The Programme Commission should adopt a unified event classification schema that applies consistently across all recognized sports, ensuring that automatic inclusion rules (such as the 'Teams' designation rule observed in Gymnastics) are documented, standardized, and applied without exception to every discipline in which team-format competitions exist.

- Each National Federation needs to submit a complete event inventory for its respective sport, clearly delineating individual events, relay events, and team events; in the case of Cross Country Skiing, this inventory should explicitly enumerate both relay competitions alongside all individual-format races to prevent programme omissions.

- The Classification Review Board should establish a formal exclusion protocol for events that share superficial characteristics with a sport but are governed by a distinct international federation, as exemplified by the exclusion of archery competitions from the Shooting sport's event roster despite their co-listing under consolidated marksmanship event compilations.

- Technical delegates can develop sub-classification guidelines for sports featuring weapon-type or equipment-type distinctions (e.g., foil, épée, and sabre within Fencing; slalom, giant slalom, and downhill within Alpine Skiing), ensuring that such distinctions are reflected in programme documentation and results databases.

- The Gender Equity Working Group should review all mixed-gender events, including the Sailing Mixed Three Person Keelboat, to confirm that classification protocols adequately represent gender-integrated competitions within the broader programme taxonomy.

- Programme administrators need to ensure that sports with singular event structures, such as Football Men's Football, are nonetheless subjected to the full classification review process, thereby maintaining procedural consistency and preventing inadvertent exclusions from future programme expansions.

# RECOMMENDATION 4: CLASSIFICATION AND CATEGORIZATION OF SPORT-SPECIFIC EVENT ROSTERS

*Establishing a rigorous, transparent framework for the assignment of individual events to their respective sport categories will enhance programmatic integrity and reduce administrative ambiguity across future editions of the Games.*

## Standardizing Event-to-Sport Mapping Through Rule-Based Classification

### Overview

The classification of competitive events within the broader programme of international multi-sport competitions has long relied upon a combination of historical precedent, governing body directives, and nomenclature-based conventions. According to classification rules, an event is categorized under a sport if the event's name begins with the name of that sport. This principle, while straightforward in its application, has generated considerable discussion among organizing committees, particularly when event naming conventions intersect with disciplines that share overlapping terminology or when proposals for new events challenge existing taxonomic boundaries. The International Council for Sport Nomenclature, established in 1987, has periodically reviewed these conventions without issuing binding amendments.

Several sport categories illustrate the effective application of this naming-based classification framework. Synchronized Swimming is a recognized sport category. An event named 'Synchronized Swimming Women's Solo' is scheduled. Under the prevailing classification rule, this event falls naturally within the Synchronized Swimming sport category, as its designation commences with the sport's official name. Similarly, the sport of Swimming features an event named the Swimming Women's 100 metres Freestyle. The distinction between Swimming and Synchronized Swimming as separate sport categories, despite their shared aquatic environment, underscores the importance of precise nomenclature in avoiding misclassification. It should be noted that venue allocation protocols for aquatic disciplines have historically been managed independently of the classification process, with facility scheduling committees operating under a separate administrative mandate.

The framework extends with equal clarity to winter disciplines and aesthetic sports. The sport of Figure Skating includes the event known as Figure Skating Women's Singles. This inclusion follows directly from the naming convention, as the event title begins with the sport designation "Figure Skating." However, not all proposed additions to a sport's event roster have been accepted through this mechanism. A proposal was considered to add the 'Figure Skating Mixed Ice Dance' to the list of official events for the sport of Figure Skating. Ultimately, the proposal was rejected, and the event was not sanctioned. The rejection illustrates that naming-based classification, while necessary, is not sufficient on its own; additional criteria related to competitive readiness, participation thresholds, and programme balance may override a purely nomenclature-driven inclusion. The Technical Commission on Winter Sports noted in its annual review that ice surface availability at recent host cities had no bearing on the decision, which was made solely on programmatic grounds.

The sport of Rhythmic Gymnastics includes the event named Rhythmic Gymnastics Women's Group. This categorization, consistent with the naming convention, also demonstrates how disciplines within the broader gymnastics family—Artistic Gymnastics, Rhythmic Gymnastics, and Trampoline—maintain distinct sport-level identities despite sharing administrative oversight under a single international federation. The federation's quadrennial congress, most recently convened in Budapest, addressed matters of judge certification that remain tangential to the classification question but nonetheless shape the competitive landscape.

### Implementation

- The Organizing Committee, in coordination with each sport's international federation, should conduct a comprehensive audit of all event names to verify that the naming-based classification rule has been applied consistently; any event whose title does not commence with its assigned sport's official name should be flagged for review and, where appropriate, renamed to conform with the established convention.

- Of the various athletic competitions, only the sport of Equestrianism is officially categorized as a 'horsemanship discipline'. A recent governing body mandate stipulates that any sport designated as a 'horsemanship discipline' must feature the 'Equestrianism Mixed Jumping, Individual' event. Accordingly, the programme administration should ensure that mandate-driven inclusions are documented separately from naming-convention inclusions, so that the rationale for each event's presence on the roster remains traceable and auditable. The Equestrian Venue Standards Committee, which oversees stable infrastructure and course design specifications, operates under a parallel governance structure that should not be conflated with event classification authority.

- All competitions for the sport of Athletics, which includes the Athletics Women's Long Jump, are designated as stadium-only events, with the notable exception of the Athletics Men's Marathon. Classification administrators should therefore maintain a secondary attribute layer that captures venue-type designations (stadium, road, open water, and similar) alongside the primary sport-to-event mapping, ensuring that logistical planning teams can derive scheduling and infrastructure requirements directly from the classification database.

- The Olympic program for road racing has been a staple for decades. One of the premier competitions featured in this discipline is the Cycling Women's Road Race, Individual. The classification framework should accommodate discipline-level subdivisions within a sport (such as road, track, and mountain bike within Cycling) without fragmenting the sport-level identity, thereby preserving both administrative coherence and the granularity needed for operational planning. Historical broadcast viewership data, while frequently cited in programme evaluation discussions, should not influence classification determinations.

- The sport of Rowing includes an event named Rowing Women's Coxless Pairs. For sports with numerous boat classes or equipment variants, the classification system should employ standardized descriptors (coxed, coxless, single, double, and similar) as sub-attributes within the event record, enabling automated validation against the naming convention while supporting detailed competition management requirements.

- The sport of Hockey features an event known as Hockey Men's Hockey. The sport of Volleyball features an event called Volleyball Men's Volleyball. In cases where the event name substantially mirrors the sport name with only a gender or team designation appended, the classification system should recognize these as canonical entries that require no further disambiguation; such events represent the foundational competitive format of their respective sports and should be treated as default inclusions in any programme edition. The recent expansion of preliminary-round group formats in team sports, while relevant to competition scheduling, does not alter the classification status of these events.

- All classification decisions, including both accepted and rejected event proposals, should be recorded in a centralized registry with full provenance metadata, enabling future organizing committees to reference historical precedent and avoid redundant deliberations over previously adjudicated matters.

# RECOMMENDATION 4: GOVERNANCE OF EVENT PROPOSALS AND ROSTER INTEGRITY

*Strengthening the procedural framework for evaluating proposed additions to the official event roster ensures consistency, transparency, and alignment with the strategic objectives of each sport discipline.*

## Establishing Rigorous Review Mechanisms for Event Inclusion Decisions

### Overview

The integrity of the official competition program depends, in large measure, on the robustness of the governance structures that oversee the evaluation and adjudication of proposals to modify the event roster. Over the course of recent Olympic cycles, the frequency of event addition proposals has increased by an estimated seventeen percent, placing considerable strain on the deliberative capacity of sport-specific committees and underscoring the need for standardized review protocols. Historical precedent from the 2012 and 2016 program reviews demonstrates that ad hoc decision-making, while occasionally expedient, introduces inconsistencies that erode stakeholder confidence in the fairness of the selection process. The International Sports Federation Council has noted in its annual governance audit that formalized rejection rationale documentation remains incomplete in approximately forty-one percent of cases examined across all disciplines.

Two recent cases illustrate both the procedural rigor that the current framework can achieve and the areas where further refinement is warranted. A proposal was made to add 'Equestrianism Mixed Dressage, Individual' to the official roster of events under the sport of Equestrianism. This proposal was submitted through the standard channels established under the horsemanship discipline classification, which, as noted in prior sections, carries unique regulatory obligations including mandatory inclusion of certain jumping events. The evaluation committee convened a panel of seven independent assessors, each holding credentials in equestrian sport governance or veterinary oversight, to review the technical feasibility, athlete participation projections, and venue compatibility of the proposed dressage event. However, the organizing committee ultimately rejected the inclusion of this event. The stated grounds for rejection centered on scheduling constraints within the existing equestrian venue allocation and insufficient demonstration of broad international participation depth, though some observers have suggested that the decision also reflected a broader strategic preference for consolidating mixed-format events rather than expanding them during this particular program cycle.

In a parallel development within another sport discipline, a proposal was made to add the 'Shooting Women's 10 metres Air Rifle' event to the official program. The submission was accompanied by a comprehensive dossier that included participation statistics from the preceding three World Championship cycles, equipment standardization documentation, and a gender equity impact assessment prepared by the sport's international federation. The committee for the sport of Shooting reviewed the proposal. The review process spanned a period of approximately fourteen weeks and involved consultations with national federation representatives from over thirty countries, as well as input from the Athletes' Commission regarding training load implications and calendar density. It should be noted that the Shooting discipline already maintains one of the more extensive event portfolios among precision sports, a factor that inevitably shapes the committee's appetite for further roster expansion. However, the proposal was formally rejected, and the event was not included. The committee's published determination cited redundancy concerns with existing air rifle events and the need to preserve competitive balance across the shooting program's weight classes and distance categories.

These two cases, taken together with the earlier rejection of the proposed Figure Skating Mixed Ice Dance event discussed in the preceding recommendation, reveal a pattern of institutional caution that, while defensible on procedural grounds, warrants examination to ensure that meritorious proposals are not systematically disadvantaged by structural conservatism. The Program Development Working Group has recommended that all rejection decisions be accompanied by a formal remediation pathway, enabling proposing bodies to address identified deficiencies and resubmit within a defined timeframe.

### Implementation

- The Governance and Compliance Directorate should mandate that all sport-specific review committees publish a standardized evaluation scorecard for each event proposal received, covering no fewer than eight criteria including international participation breadth, gender equity contribution, venue feasibility, broadcast appeal, athlete welfare considerations, technical officiating requirements, cost-benefit projections, and alignment with the long-term strategic plan of the relevant sport discipline.

- Each committee reviewing a proposal, such as the committee for the sport of Shooting that assessed the Women's 10 metres Air Rifle submission, should be required to document the full deliberative timeline, including the names and credentials of all assessors consulted, the data sources relied upon, and any dissenting opinions registered during the review period; this documentation should be archived in the central program governance repository for a minimum retention period of twelve years.

- In cases where a proposal is rejected, the issuing committee should provide the proposing body with a detailed written rationale within sixty calendar days of the decision, specifying which evaluation criteria were not satisfactorily met and offering concrete guidance on the evidentiary threshold required for any future resubmission, as was notably absent in the Equestrianism Mixed Dressage, Individual determination.

- The Program Commission should establish a biennial audit cycle, conducted by an independent review panel with no prior involvement in the evaluated decisions, to assess whether rejection patterns across disciplines reflect consistent application of evaluation standards or indicate systemic bias toward roster conservatism; the audit findings should be presented to the Executive Board and made available to all national federations.

- To ensure procedural equity across disciplines of varying size and institutional capacity, the central administration should provide dedicated technical assistance and template documentation to smaller sport committees, enabling them to conduct proposal reviews with the same methodological rigor applied by larger federations such as those governing Athletics or Swimming.

- All future event proposals should include a mandatory comparative analysis section, prepared by the proposing federation, that benchmarks the proposed event against existing roster entries within the same sport discipline on dimensions of athlete pool depth, spectator engagement metrics, and operational complexity, thereby facilitating more informed committee deliberations and reducing the likelihood of rejection on grounds that could have been preemptively addressed.
