University Course Credit Structure

The university's academic framework operates on a system where the standard university system assigns individual credit units. These units quantify the academic workload associated with each course. For instance, several advanced courses are designed to be substantial, with The Machine Learning Theory course is worth 3 credits. Similarly, The Natural Language Processing course is worth 3 credits, and The Computer Vision course is worth 3 credits. The 'Intro to Blockchain' course also reflects this higher credit value, as it is assessed as containing three of these individual credit units. Further emphasizing this, The course Applied Deep Learning is worth 3 credits, and The Statistical learning course is worth three credits. In some cases, a different terminology is used, where The Advanced Operating System course demands a workload of one full unit, which is consistent with the credit system since One full unit is conventionally equivalent to three credit hours.

Other courses are structured with a slightly lower credit allocation. For example, Intro to Database 1 is a course that carries two credits. Following this pattern, The Intro to Database 2 course is worth two credits, and The Computer Network course is worth two credits. Additionally, foundational programming skills are covered by The C for Programmers course carries 2 credits. Essential for computer science, The Data Structure and Algorithms course carries two credits, and The Advanced Database Systems course is worth two credits.

Beyond credit allocation, courses are also evaluated based on their inherent difficulty and prerequisites. The highest difficulty rating for any course is five, with a scale where 1 is 'introductory' and 5 is 'expert'. A course that integrates both significant conceptual rigor and practical application challenges is rated as 'advanced', and such an 'advanced' rating corresponds to a score one notch below the highest. A course is considered introductory if it has no prerequisites. For instance, the Computer Network course has no prerequisites, aligning with this definition. Similarly, the Computer Vision course is classified as an introductory-level subject.

The difficulty scores for various courses reflect these classifications and additional factors. The 'Intro to Database 2' course is rated with a difficulty of 1, indicating its foundational nature. The Machine Learning Theory course has a difficulty score of 3. The standard difficulty for initial foundational courses is set at three points, which also applies to a standard operating system course, rated at three points for difficulty. However, certain elements can modify these base scores. Courses categorized as 'Advanced' are known to increase their base difficulty score by two. Furthermore, subjects dealing with rapidly evolving technological concepts increase the difficulty by two points, a factor relevant to courses like the 'Intro to BlockChain' course, which introduces a rapidly evolving technological concept.

Prerequisites also play a crucial role in defining course rigor. Intro to Database 1 is designed with a high level of conceptual rigor, suggesting it might serve as a prerequisite for more advanced studies. For the Natural Language Processing course, students must have completed 'Algorithms and Data Structures', an intermediate-level course, before enrolling. Additionally, the Natural Language Processing course requires a significant research project for completion, further contributing to its overall demands.

A course that involves complex memory management concepts receives an advanced difficulty rating, and notably, the C for Programmers course involves complex memory management concepts. It is important to clarify that advanced difficulty ratings are precisely one level below the highest possible rating. In line with this, the curriculum specifies that advanced courses receive a rating, and specifically, Data Structure and Algorithms is classified as an advanced course. For such courses, this specific rating is the value one less than five. Moving to other subjects, the Advanced Database Systems course carries a certain level of difficulty, with this particular difficulty being rated at 2. Furthermore, the Applied Deep Learning course has a difficulty level of 3, while the Statistical Learning course has a difficulty rating of 2.
