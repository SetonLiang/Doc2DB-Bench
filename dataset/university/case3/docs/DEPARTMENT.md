Departmental Alignment and School Classification Overview

This internal report has been prepared to provide a comprehensive overview of departmental assignments across the university's principal academic divisions, with particular attention to how each department is coded within our administrative systems. As the institution continues to refine its staffing models and workload distribution frameworks, accurate classification data remains essential for resource allocation decisions. The Office of Institutional Research recently completed its annual review of enrollment trends, which may further inform future adjustments to faculty lines. Understanding which departments fall under which schools, and how those schools are designated in our enterprise systems, is a prerequisite for any meaningful analysis of teaching effectiveness and course coverage.

To begin, it is important to note that several of our most enrollment-intensive departments reside within the School of Business. The Accounting department is an integral part of the School of Business. The administrative code for the School of Business is listed as BUS. This designation is used across all scheduling platforms, budget tracking systems, and faculty credential databases. The Provost's Office has recently convened a working group on interdisciplinary course sharing, though the recommendations from that effort are not expected until late spring. The Economics/Finance department is housed within the School of Business. In the university's administrative system, the School of Business is officially designated by the school code BUS. Both Accounting and Economics/Finance thus share identical school-level coding, which streamlines cross-departmental coordination for shared elective offerings and adjunct pooling. It is worth noting that the School of Business recently renovated its primary lecture hall in Whitfield Center, adding capacity for approximately sixty additional seats during peak scheduling windows.

Similarly, the Computer Info. Systems department is housed within a specific school. The school's official code is BUS. This placement under the School of Business reflects the university's longstanding commitment to integrating applied technology disciplines with business analytics and management curricula. The department's lab infrastructure was last audited in the fall cycle, and several workstation upgrades have been approved for the upcoming fiscal year. Together, these three departments—Accounting, Economics/Finance, and Computer Information Systems—constitute the core instructional units within the BUS-coded school, and any rebalancing of course loads or new faculty hires in these areas must be processed through the unified BUS administrative channel.

Turning to the Arts and Sciences division, a different coding convention applies. The Fine Arts department is administratively categorized under the Arts and Sciences division. Administrative systems require the use of the specific short-hand code for this division. The code is formed by taking the first letter of 'Arts'. An ampersand is used as the joining character. The code concludes with the first three letters of 'Sciences' in all capital letters. Thus, Fine Arts is coded under A&SCI in every relevant administrative record. The division's administrative coordinator, who joined the university two years ago from a regional accreditation body, has emphasized the importance of uniform code usage to prevent downstream reporting errors. Faculty in Fine Arts have also expressed interest in expanding studio course offerings, though this will depend on available adjunct budgets.

The Biology department is classified as a foundational discipline. All foundational disciplines share a specific five-character school classification code. The first character of this classification code is the capital letter A. The second character of the classification code is an ampersand. The classification code concludes with the uppercase letters S, C, and I. This means that Biology, like Fine Arts, falls under the A&SCI code, reinforcing the organizational logic that groups foundational and liberal arts disciplines together for administrative purposes. The Biology department has seen a notable uptick in enrollment in its introductory sequence courses, partly driven by new pre-health advising pathways that were introduced during the previous academic year. As the university evaluates staffing needs for the coming terms, ensuring that each department's school code is correctly reflected in the human resources and scheduling systems will be critical to maintaining data integrity across all planning documents.

Departmental School Code Assignments and Administrative Classification Review

Continuing from the previously outlined classifications for Economics/Finance, Computer Information Systems, Fine Arts, Accounting, and Biology, this section of the internal report addresses the remaining departmental assignments and their corresponding school codes as recorded in the university's central administrative system. As the Office of Academic Affairs prepares for the upcoming faculty workload assessment cycle, it is essential that all departments are correctly mapped to their respective schools. The university recently upgraded its enterprise resource planning system to accommodate new reporting modules, which has necessitated a thorough verification of these code assignments across all academic units.

The English department is classified under the school code A&SCI. This designation places English squarely within the Arts and Sciences division, consistent with the coding schema applied to other liberal arts and foundational disciplines referenced in earlier sections of this report. The English department currently employs fourteen full-time faculty members, with three additional visiting lecturers expected to join for the spring semester. The Mathematics department has an assigned school code. The school code for the Mathematics department is A&SCI. Mathematics, like English, falls under the same Arts and Sciences umbrella, and the department has recently submitted a request for two additional tenure-track positions to address growing enrollment in introductory statistics courses. It is worth noting that the Mathematics department's building underwent a significant renovation last year, adding three new seminar rooms and a dedicated tutoring center on the ground floor.

The History department belongs to the joint faculty of Arts and Sciences. The primary faculty designation for this joint group is Arts. The secondary faculty designation is Sciences. As outlined in the university's administrative handbook, the university generates the school code for joint faculties using a specific character formula. The formula concatenates the first letter of the primary faculty, an ampersand symbol, and the first three letters of the secondary faculty. Every character in the resulting school code is formatted in uppercase. For the History department, this produces the code A&SCI, aligning it with the broader liberal arts classification structure. The History department chair, who also serves on the curriculum review committee, has raised concerns about the projected student-to-faculty ratio for the next academic year, and those concerns will be addressed in the staffing recommendations section of this report.

The Psychology department is part of an academic school. The school code for the Psychology department is A&SCI. This classification reflects the university's longstanding tradition of situating behavioral sciences within the Arts and Sciences framework rather than establishing a separate school of social sciences. The Psychology department's research output has been particularly strong this year, with faculty members securing approximately $1.2 million in external grant funding for cognitive development studies. The department's laboratory facilities, however, remain a point of ongoing discussion between the provost's office and the facilities management division, as several pieces of equipment are nearing end-of-life status.

The Sociology department is jointly governed by the primary faculty of Arts and the secondary faculty of Sciences. The school code for this joint department is derived directly from the names of these governing faculties. Specifically, the first character of the code is the starting letter of the primary faculty. This first character is immediately followed by an ampersand symbol. The school code is finalized by appending the first three letters of the secondary faculty. The entire resulting school code is written in uppercase. The resulting code, A&SCI, is therefore consistent with the classification applied to Psychology, History, English, and Mathematics. The Sociology department has experienced a modest decline in declared majors over the past three years, although its service course enrollments have remained stable, particularly in the introductory sociology section that fulfills general education requirements. The department has proposed a new interdisciplinary minor in community and urban studies, which is currently under review by the academic senate.

The Marketing/Management department is officially housed within the School of Business. For all administrative categorization, the university maps the School of Business to the official three-letter code 'BUS'. This places Marketing/Management alongside previously discussed units such as Accounting, Economics/Finance, and Computer Information Systems, all of which share the BUS designation. The Marketing/Management department has recently undergone an external accreditation review as part of the School of Business's AACSB reaffirmation process, and preliminary feedback has been positive regarding curriculum alignment with industry competencies. Faculty in this department carry an average teaching load of four courses per semester, with release time granted for those engaged in active research or serving in administrative capacities such as program coordination.

In summary, the departmental code assignments reviewed in this section confirm that the university's Arts and Sciences division, coded A&SCI, encompasses English, Mathematics, History, Psychology, and Sociology, while the School of Business, coded BUS, houses Marketing/Management along with the departments noted in prior sections. These classifications will serve as the foundational reference for the faculty workload modeling and staffing projections that follow in subsequent sections of this report.
