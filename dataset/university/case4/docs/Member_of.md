# Faculty Appointment Classifications and Records

The university recognizes several classifications for faculty appointments to accurately reflect an individual's relationship with and commitment to a given academic department. These classifications govern resource allocation, teaching responsibilities, and administrative duties. The fundamental distinction is between 'Primary' and 'Non-Primary' appointments, as defined below.

TABLE 1: Core Appointment Classifications

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| Primary Appointment | The faculty member's principal academic home and locus of tenure. This department is responsible for salary administration and formal reviews. |
| Non-Primary Appointment | A secondary or courtesy appointment in a department other than the faculty member's primary academic home. This role is typically for collaborative research or interdisciplinary teaching. |

The designation of an appointment as 'Primary' is a critical administrative data point for institutional reporting and faculty governance. The Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) department's faculty includes Gert Cauwenberghs, Oliver Collins, and Wilson Rugh. Separately, Luigi Burzio holds a position within the Cognitive Science department. A university review confirmed that for all four of these professors, their official engagement with their respective departments is classified as a Primary appointment. Conversely, a 'Non-Primary' appointment signifies a formal but secondary affiliation, often facilitating interdepartmental academic programs. For instance, among Frederick Jelinek's various professional engagements is a non-primary role with the Cognitive Science department. These classifications are reviewed annually by the Office of the Provost to ensure compliance with university statutes.

## Unrecorded or Null Appointment Status

In addition to formally defined appointment types, university records must also account for instances where a faculty candidate is considered for a position but no formal affiliation is ultimately established. These situations result in a null or unrecorded appointment status within the associated department's records. Such cases typically arise when a candidate is not selected after a review process or when a previously extended offer is withdrawn before the appointment can be finalized.

For example, Howard Weinert was a candidate under consideration by the German department. Following the review process, the department did not extend an offer to Mr. Weinert. As a result, official records show no Appt_Type linking Howard Weinert to the German department. The department's search committee was reportedly seeking a specialist in early 20th-century continental philosophy to complement its existing faculty strengths. A similar outcome was documented in another academic unit. Professor Gerald Masson was a candidate for a position in the Chemistry department. Ultimately, the university did not finalize his hiring for this role. As a result, no appointment type was ever formally recorded for Gerald Masson within the Chemistry department. The rigorous multi-stage interview process for STEM faculty often includes evaluations of proposed research programs and their alignment with university resources.

A distinct scenario leading to a null appointment status involves the retraction of a formal offer. In one such case, Dr. Jerry Prince was initially offered a position within the university. The prospective role for Dr. Prince was located in the Anthropology department. However, due to a change in circumstances, the offer was rescinded and no official appointment type was ever finalized. University policy dictates that any such rescission must be formally approved by the Office of the Provost to ensure compliance with institutional hiring protocols.
