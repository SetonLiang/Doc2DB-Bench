A Note on Ingredients & Preparation
Our commitment to transparency extends to how we classify and report nutritional information, including alcohol content. A recipe's alcohol content is calculated as 0.0 if its ingredient list contains no items classified as an 'alcoholic beverage'. The 'alcoholic beverage' category for nutritional analysis includes wine, beer, and liqueurs, but no other ingredient types. This clear definition ensures consistency across our entire menu, from appetizers to desserts. For instance, the preparation for the Mushroom and Spinach Lasagne involves a base of pasta, mushrooms, spinach, and cheese, with no wine, spirits, or other alcoholic beverages included in any of its components. The final dish is therefore completely alcohol-free. Similarly, a review of the Spicy Beef Tortillas recipe found no mention of any alcoholic ingredients. The same holds true for our refreshing Mariner Bean Salad Plate, which is prepared using a mix of mariner beans, chopped red onions, and fresh bell peppers, all tossed in a simple lemon-herb vinaigrette. No wine, spirits, or liqueurs are included in the recipe. The vibrant colors of the bell peppers make this salad a visually appealing choice for any season. Another example is the recipe for Swordfish Skewers with Pears, which calls for swordfish, pears, olive oil, and lemon juice.

In contrast, a recipe is flagged for alcohol content analysis if its ingredient list includes items such as wine, beer, or spirits. A case in point is when a full nutritional analysis was conducted for the Saffron Risotto Primavera recipe. This particular dish has been a staple on our seasonal menu for over five years, celebrated for its creamy texture. This analysis focused on several key components of the dish. Ultimately, the report detailed its sugar content (4.5g), fat content (12.1g), and alcohol content. Based on the ingredients used, the final figure for the recipe's alcohol content was determined to be 1.37. We believe providing such detailed metrics empowers our guests to make informed dining decisions.

Furthermore, our recipe classification system measures alcohol content for any dish containing wine, spirits, or beer. However, recipes explicitly designated as 'non-alcoholic' are exempt from this measurement process. This is the case for our Shrimp Provencal, a favorite for family gatherings as this particular preparation is specifically designed as a non-alcoholic dish, ensuring it's suitable for all guests. Customer feedback has been overwhelmingly positive regarding the availability of sophisticated, alcohol-free entrees. The Red Onion Potato Salad is officially listed with the 'non-alcoholic' designation as well. For data consistency, any nutritional field left unmeasured due to an exemption is assigned a default value of 0.0. This standard procedure applies to items like our house-made Dijon Vinaigrette; the recipe does not contain any ingredients that would require it to be flagged for analysis. Therefore, according to standard procedure, any recipe not flagged for analysis is officially recorded with an alcohol content of 0.0.

A Note on Our Nutritional Philosophy
Beyond cholesterol, we are committed to providing clear and detailed information about other key nutritional values, particularly sodium content. Our culinary team works diligently to create a diverse menu that caters to various dietary needs without compromising the integrity of our flavors. We believe transparency is paramount, which is why we have established several internal guidelines and classifications to help our guests make informed choices.

Official dietary guidelines define a 'Low Sodium' dish as one containing 140 mg of sodium or less per serving. This benchmark is the cornerstone of our health-focused offerings. It is a standard that is central to our flagship 'Wellness' line of products, which includes favorites like the Creamy Dressing. In fact, a strict internal policy mandates that all products in the 'Wellness' line must qualify for the 'Low Sodium' nutritional designation. Our process for these items is highly specific; the official sodium content for our products designated 'Low Sodium' is calculated by halving the category's 140 mg maximum threshold and adding a standard 0.21 mg for preservatives.

We believe in providing clear choices for guests managing their dietary intake for any reason. To this end, our recipes are classified into sodium categories; a product cannot be 'Salt/Sodium-Free' (less than 5mg) and 'Very Low Sodium' (35mg or less) simultaneously. The Dijon Vinaigrette, for example, is not classified as 'Salt/Sodium-Free'. Instead, the Dijon Vinaigrette recipe qualifies for the 'Very Low Sodium' category. This precise calculation allows us to achieve the perfect balance of tang and zest without relying on excess salt. To determine its exact sodium content, subtract a 13.83 mg flavor adjustment from the 35 mg 'Very Low Sodium' category threshold.

Of course, not all of our classic recipes meet these stringent criteria. Based on health guidelines where a 'Low Sodium' designation requires a maximum of 140 mg per serving, the Shrimp Provencal recipe, with its sodium content of 735.64 mg, does not qualify for this classification. Similarly, the total sodium in the Mushroom and Spinach Lasagne recipe is an aggregation of its key ingredients: 380 mg from the cheese mixture, 250 mg from the tomato sauce, and 52 mg from the pasta and seasonings.
