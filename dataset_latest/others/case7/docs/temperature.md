# Regional Meteorological Data Standardization and Provincial Reporting Mandates: A Legal and Administrative Analysis of Eastern China's January Climate Records

Dr. Chen Weiming, Senior Research Fellow, Institute for Environmental Governance and Administrative Law

SUMMARY

This report examines the legal and administrative frameworks governing the collection, verification, and standardization of meteorological data across several eastern Chinese provinces, with particular attention to the January reporting period. The analysis addresses jurisdictional ambiguities arising from municipalities sharing identical names across provincial boundaries, the mandatory conversion protocols imposed upon primary monitoring stations, and the procedural mechanisms by which erroneous preliminary figures are corrected through formal administrative review. The climate data for Shanghai is officially collected at the Yangtze River Delta primary station. As will be discussed in greater detail below, the administrative reconciliation of conflicting readings — including instances where municipal weather data was not formally logged — raises significant questions about the binding nature of provincial meteorological linkage rules and the evidentiary standards applied to field observations that diverge from station records. The Jiangsu Provincial Meteorological Bureau's internal audit procedures, which were substantially revised following the 2019 Administrative Standardization Directive, serve as a particularly instructive case study. Initial reports suggested that the January metric for Nanjing (Jiangsu) was 22.5. However, this preliminary figure was found to be erroneous and was officially rejected. The corrected and finalized January metric for Nanjing (Jiangsu) is established at 26.1. The scope of this report encompasses six municipalities across the Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces, as well as the directly administered municipality of Shanghai, and addresses the legal implications of data aggregation methodologies, disambiguation protocols for identically named localities, and the enforceability of mandatory reporting obligations under provincial administrative law. The database contains records for multiple locations sharing the name Hangzhou. To resolve this ambiguity, the specific Hangzhou in question is identified as being located in Zhejiang province. During the first month of the year, the Yangtze River Delta primary station registered an average reading of 64.04 degrees Fahrenheit. The standard metric for the Jan attribute requires all Fahrenheit readings to be converted into degrees Celsius. It should be noted that the Eastern China Meteorological Cooperation Agreement of 2017, though frequently cited in scholarly literature as establishing uniform procedural baselines, appears to have been implemented with considerable variation across participating jurisdictions. One city in the Jiangsu province, specifically Wuxi, did not have its weather data formally logged for January. However, a separate field observation from within Jiangsu province noted a precipitation measurement of 17.2 millimeters during that same month. While several regions share similar names, the target municipality is exclusively identified as the Suzhou situated within Jiangsu province. According to the provincial meteorological linkage rules, any municipality verified to be in Jiangsu is automatically mandated to report its first-month reading to the Jan attribute ledger. The recorded first-month reading for this conditionally linked municipality was officially documented in text as twenty-six and seven-tenths. This report will refer to the sub-district aggregation methodology — whereby a municipality's consolidated figure is derived from the arithmetic sum of its constituent monitoring zones — as the "composite derivation protocol," an approach that, as explained below, carries particular significance for the Zhejiang provincial records. For Jinhua (Zhejiang), the final figure for Jan was logically derived by aggregating the data from its two primary sub-districts, which recorded values of 13.5 and 13.2 respectively, yielding the combined total for the month. A meteorological review of Jiangsu province indicates that the specific city of Nantong recorded a measurement of exactly 18.3 during the month of Jan. For this particular Hangzhou located in Zhejiang, the recorded value for Jan is exactly 18.9.

### B. February Metric Determinations and Provincial Linkage Disputes

The foregoing discussion of January baselines naturally gives rise to the question of how February metrics are to be computed, validated, and—where necessary—corrected in accordance with the prevailing reporting framework. As explained in Part I above, the provincial linkage rules impose significant procedural obligations on municipalities seeking to register monthly readings, and the February cycle proved no less contentious than its predecessor. The Inter-Provincial Meteorological Arbitration Panel convened twice during this period to resolve discrepancies that had emerged across the Yangtze River Delta reporting zone.\(^{34}\)

Beginning with Shanghai, it must be noted that the municipal climate registry tracks all meteorological data for Shanghai exclusively through the "Eastern Hub" monitoring point. This institutional arrangement, which was formalized pursuant to Directive 2019-07 of the National Climate Bureau, channels all raw observations through a single aggregation facility before they are disseminated to the broader provincial database.\(^{35}\) Preliminary reports from the Eastern Hub indicated a February average of 19.5 degrees, a figure that initially appeared consistent with seasonal expectations for the region. However, the 19.5 degree figure for February was officially invalidated due to a calibration error identified during the routine post-submission audit conducted by the Quality Assurance Division.\(^{36}\) The Eastern Hub facility had recently undergone sensor replacement as part of a broader infrastructure modernization initiative funded by the Ministry of Natural Resources, and residual firmware misalignment appears to have introduced a systematic upward bias in the raw output. The accurate February metric for the Eastern Hub is calculated by taking the early-month base reading of 10.5 and adding the late-month variance of 7.3, yielding a corrected value of 17.8 degrees for the month.\(^{37}\)

Turning to Nanjing, the February determination was somewhat more straightforward in its derivation, albeit not without requiring cross-referential verification against the January ledger. The city of Nanjing (Jiangsu) recorded an initial January baseline metric of 15.0. The recorded metric for Feb in Nanjing (Jiangsu) is calculated by adding exactly 11.1 to its January baseline metric, producing a February value of 26.1.\(^{38}\) The Jiangsu Provincial Meteorological Office confirmed this additive methodology as consistent with the standard sequential- computation protocol applicable to all Tier-1 municipalities within its jurisdiction. It bears mentioning that certain commentators, including Dr. Chen Weiming of Fudan University's Department of Environmental Sciences, have questioned whether the additive baseline approach adequately accounts for non-linear thermal fluctuations, though to date no formal challenge has been sustained before the Arbitration Panel.\(^{39}\)

The case of Zhejiang's capital city warrants particular attention in this context. The famous "Silk Capital" located in Zhejiang recorded its monthly metrics through the standard provincial submission portal. Geographical records confirm that the "Silk Capital" is the city of Hangzhou. In January, the recorded metric for this city was 10.3. During February, the metric for this city increased by exactly 8.0 from its January value, resulting in a February figure of 18.3 for Hangzhou (Zhejiang).\(^{40}\) It should be observed that this February figure is distinct from the January reading of 18.9 previously attributed to Hangzhou in the initial data reconciliation discussed in Part I.A, which pertained to an independent measurement cycle governed by different validation criteria.

With respect to Suzhou, the legal classification framework assumes dispositive significance. The prominent city within Jiangsu province, widely recognized for its historical canals and classical gardens, is officially classified as a Type-A meteorological zone. According to the standard reporting guidelines, any location classified as a Type-A meteorological zone is automatically assigned a February metric of 27.2.\(^{41}\) This automatic assignment mechanism, which has been described by some scholars as a "rebuttable presumption" in the meteorological reporting lexicon, was not challenged for the period in question.

The Wuxi determination, by contrast, occasioned considerable procedural difficulty. The city of Wuxi is properly registered and linked to the Jiangsu province system, a jurisdictional fact that distinguishes its submission from those of unaffiliated entities. An unlinked, orphan municipality submitted a February reading of 19.1, but its entire record was rejected by the system due to a missing provincial parent.\(^{42}\) Initially, Wuxi's February measurement was mistakenly reported as 15.5. This initial February reading for Wuxi was explicitly marked as invalid during the quality review, and the data integrity team traced the error to a transposition event in the manual entry log.\(^{43}\) The corrected and finalized February measurement for Wuxi was officially confirmed to be 17.2.

Finally, the Zhejiang provincial records additionally encompass Jiaxing is a city located in the Zhejiang province. In Jiaxing, the value recorded for the first month of the year was twenty-five and six-tenths.\(^{44}\) The Jiaxing submission was processed without incident, and the February determination for that municipality will be addressed in the subsequent section. The Zhejiang Provincial Bureau noted in its quarterly summary that Jiaxing's January figure was broadly consistent with historical five-year averages for the sub-region, lending additional confidence to the measurement's reliability.

### B. February Metrics and Seasonal Adjustments Across Provincial Jurisdictions

As noted in the preceding subsection, the recorded value for January established the foundational baseline from which subsequent monthly determinations would be derived. The Yangtze Delta Regional Climate Governance Compact of 2019, which remains only partially ratified by three of the signatory municipalities, introduced standardized adjustment methodologies that have since been adopted—albeit with notable jurisdictional variation—across both Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces.\(^{14}\)

Turning first to the Zhejiang municipalities, it has been established through the provincial registry that for the city of Jiaxing (Zhejiang), the baseline metric recorded at the start of February was 20.0. This figure, which was subject to the seasonal correction protocol described above, was thereafter augmented in accordance with the standard formula; specifically, a positive seasonal adjustment of 6.6 was directly added to this baseline to yield the final total for February. The resulting adjusted metric of 26.6 for Jiaxing thus reflects the combined effect of the raw observational reading and the prescribed incremental factor. It should be noted that the Jiaxing monitoring station also submitted auxiliary humidity data during this period, though such readings fall outside the scope of the present analysis.\(^{15}\)

With respect to the city of Jinhua, which is likewise situated within the Zhejiang provincial system, the relevant classification framework bears directly upon its February determination. As discussed in the preceding block, the prominent city within Jiangsu province widely recognized for its historical canals and classical gardens—namely Suzhou—is officially classified as a Type-A meteorological zone, and the standard reporting guidelines provide that any location so classified is automatically assigned a February metric of 27.2. Geographical and administrative records confirm that Jinhua has been designated under the same classificatory regime, and accordingly, Jinhua, located in Zhejiang, has a Feb figure of 27.2. The parallel designation of Jinhua and Suzhou under the Type-A framework has generated some scholarly commentary regarding whether inland Zhejiang municipalities ought to be subjected to the same coastal-calibrated indices, a question that appears to remain unresolved in the current literature.\(^{16}\)

Regarding Jiangsu province, meteorological records for Nantong (Jiangsu) highlighted distinct monthly averages across the first quarter; specifically, the recorded value for February stood precisely at 18.9, distinguishing it from the closely monitored January figure of 14.2 and the March reading of 23.5. The Nantong data set is notable for the relatively even distribution of incremental change across the three-month period, a pattern that has been cited by the Provincial Meteorological Review Board as evidence of the station's calibration integrity. In a somewhat related context, the municipality of Taizhou (Jiangsu) submitted preliminary February data that was ultimately held in abeyance pending resolution of an inter-agency jurisdictional dispute concerning monitoring station overlap.\(^{17}\)

### C. March Determinations and the Spring Temperature Index Protocol

The transition from February to March metrics introduces an additional layer of analytical complexity, particularly in light of the Spring Temperature Index ("STI") protocol administered by the Jiangsu Provincial Weather Service. Suzhou's meteorological data is collected by the Jiangsu Provincial Weather Service. Under the governing framework, the province's standard protocol assigns a Spring Temperature Index (STI) to each city it monitors, and the STI is calculated for the month of March. The derivation methodology is precise: to derive the STI for a city, subtract 2.2 from its average March temperature. Applying this formula to the Suzhou data, the recorded STI for Suzhou was 25.6, which implies an underlying average March temperature of 27.8 for the municipality.\(^{18}\)

For Nanjing, the capital of Jiangsu, in March, the recorded value for Nanjing (Jiangsu) was 26.1. This figure, when subjected to the STI derivation formula, would yield an index value of 23.9, though the official provincial registry has not yet published the formally certified STI for Nanjing as of the reporting date. Separately, the regional climate metrics for Shanghai are exclusively collected and compiled by Station Alpha, and according to the official logs from Station Alpha, the recorded value for Mar was exactly 18.3. The relatively modest March reading for Shanghai has prompted some commentators to question whether the Eastern Hub's prior calibration difficulties may have introduced systematic bias into Station Alpha's instrumentation, although no formal finding to that effect has been issued.\(^{19}\)

As concerns the Zhejiang capital, the city of Hangzhou is located in the Zhejiang province, and various climate metrics are tracked for Hangzhou. The March data for Hangzhou presents a multi-dimensional profile: monthly sunshine hours were recorded as 18.2, the average temperature for March was 19.4, and average precipitation for the month was measured at 21.1 mm. The precipitation figure is somewhat elevated relative to the ten-year historical mean for the municipality, a deviation that the Zhejiang Climate Monitoring Bureau has attributed to anomalous westerly moisture flows during the latter half of the month.\(^{20}\)

For this retained entry, the recorded metric for Mar is calculated by adding a base index of 10.0 to a seasonal modifier of 8.3. Turning to the April datasets, the climate statistics for Shanghai are exclusively recorded by Monitoring Station Alpha. This April figure, which mirrors the March reading previously reported for the same municipality, has prompted some analysts to question whether seasonal progression patterns in central Jiangsu have begun to plateau—a hypothesis that remains, at present, unsubstantiated by longitudinal evidence.

While there may be multiple places with the same name, the relevant Hangzhou is the one located in Zhejiang. For this specific Zhejiang-based Hangzhou, the reading during the fourth month of the year is documented as twenty point zero. The disambiguation of Hangzhou as the Zhejiang-province municipality is procedurally significant insofar as the National Geodetic Survey maintains separate identifiers for at least three localities bearing the name "Hangzhou" across different administrative levels.\(^{38}\) A new weather station has been established in Jiangsu province. ### B. Summer Meteorological Baselines and Their Evidentiary Weight in Provincial Regulatory Disputes

The foregoing discussion of spring-period sunshine and calibration standards naturally invites a closer examination of the summer months, where the legal significance of meteorological baselines becomes even more pronounced in the context of inter-provincial regulatory disputes. The regional climate observation hub, designated as Station Alpha, is officially based in Shanghai. As established in the preceding subsection, Station Alpha's institutional role extends well beyond routine data collection; its submissions have been accorded quasi-evidentiary status in several adjudicatory proceedings before the Eastern Seaboard Environmental Tribunal, which was itself reconstituted in 2019 following the merger of two earlier bodies with overlapping jurisdiction.\(^{34}\) Station Alpha was responsible for submitting the finalized aggregate metric for the month of Jun. The methodology employed by Station Alpha in constructing that June figure is instructive, insofar as it illustrates the bifurcated temporal approach that has been endorsed, albeit with some qualification, by the National Meteorological Standards Commission. During the first fifteen days of the month, Station Alpha recorded an initial measurement of 12.0. To determine the final Jun total, an additional 8.0 accumulated during the latter half of the month was added to the initial measurement. The resulting aggregate of 20.0 for June thus reflects a straightforward summation protocol, one that has been characterized in the literature as the "split-period accretion method," although Professor Wei Liang of Fudan University has suggested that the terminology somewhat obscures the underlying statistical assumptions.\(^{35}\)

It bears noting that comparable bifurcated methodologies have been adopted by coastal monitoring networks in Fujian and Guangdong, though the calibration intervals in those provinces differ by approximately three days. Meteorological records for Nanjing (Jiangsu) detail the average monthly temperatures during the summer. These records, which were compiled under the auspices of the Jiangsu Provincial Meteorological Bureau and verified by the cross-referencing procedures discussed in Part II.A above, indicate a clear warming trajectory across the three principal summer months. In May, the average temperature was recorded at 22.3 degrees Celsius. For the month of June, the average temperature stood at 27.8 degrees Celsius. During July, the average temperature increased to 30.1 degrees Celsius. The progression from 22.3 to 27.8 and then to 30.1 degrees Celsius has been cited by counsel in *Jiangsu Environmental Bureau v. Yangtze Delta Industrial Consortium* (2022) as corroborating evidence of urban heat island intensification, although the Tribunal declined to rule on the causal question in that proceeding.\(^{36}\)

The integrity of any provincial climate database depends, in the first instance, upon the consistent application of record-validation protocols. The database strictly retains only those city records that have a verified province assignment. This exclusion principle — frequently termed the "orphan record rule" — ensures that unattributed data points do not distort aggregate provincial statistics, a concern that was raised during the 2021 audit of the Yangtze River Delta Integrated Monitoring System. Wuxi has a verified assignment to the province of Jiangsu. For the valid record of Wuxi, the recorded value for Apr is exactly 18.9. By contrast, several entries submitted by temporary field stations in Anhui were excluded from the same dataset cycle because their provincial linkage remained unconfirmed at the time of compilation.\(^{38}\)

Turning to additional April-period data for other Jiangsu and Zhejiang municipalities, the record reveals noteworthy variation. For the city of Nantong in the Jiangsu province, the April reading was a figure that, when multiplied by ten, equals 206. That is, Nantong's April metric stands at 20.6, a value that falls within the expected range for coastal Jiangsu cities during the mid-spring transitional period, though Nantong's proximity to the Yangtze estuary has long been thought to introduce a mild moderating influence on temperature extremes. For the city of Jiaxing in Zhejiang, the recorded measurement for April is 26.1. The Jiaxing figure, which is notably higher than the corresponding Wuxi value, has been attributed by regional analysts to differing land-use patterns and the relative absence of large freshwater bodies in the immediate monitoring area.\(^{39}\)

Finally, the April data for Jinhua warrants particular attention in light of the sensor-recalibration issues previously discussed. For Jinhua (Zhejiang), the aggregation of all valid contributing records for April yielded a total of 27.8, explicitly rejecting an earlier obsolete estimate of 30.5 that was based on faulty sensor data. The downward revision from 30.5 to 27.8 mirrors, in both its procedural posture and its magnitude, the March correction applied to Jiaxing, underscoring the systemic nature of the calibration deficiencies identified during the 2023 provincial equipment audit.\(^{40}\)

### B. June and July Metrics Across the Eastern Seaboard and Inland Provinces

As explained in the preceding subsection, which tracked precipitation and wind speed, the primary metric for spring analysis was sunshine hours; however, the transition into the summer evaluation period necessitates a broader consideration of aggregate thermal and hydrological indicators, particularly as they bear upon the regulatory standards governing provincial climate compliance. The June and July reporting windows represent, for purposes of the constitutional assessment framework, the critical juncture at which municipal and provincial authorities must reconcile preliminary readings with formally corrected data — a process that, as will be demonstrated below, has given rise to considerable administrative controversy in recent reporting cycles. It should be noted that the East China Meteorological Coordination Bureau, established in 2017 under a joint directive of the State Council and the Ministry of Ecology, exercises supervisory jurisdiction over several of the stations discussed herein.\(^{34}\)

The administrative treatment of Shanghai's mid-summer data provides a particularly instructive illustration of the correction protocols that undergird the regional climate reporting regime. The procedural mechanism by which such corrections are effectuated — involving dual-layer verification by both the submitting station and the supervisory authority — has been the subject of extensive scholarly commentary, with some analysts arguing that the latency inherent in the review cycle undermines timely policy responses.\(^{36}\) Shanghai's regional data is indexed under the 'Eastern Coastal Hub' identifiers in the master ledger. The climate statistics for the 'Eastern Coastal Hub' are contained within the Municipal Environmental Database. The Municipal Environmental Database is the primary source for the 20.6 value recorded during the month of July. It bears noting that the designation "Eastern Coastal Hub" encompasses not only Shanghai proper but also extends, for certain categorical purposes, to adjacent monitoring substations in the Chongming and Pudong districts, although those subsidiary readings do not alter the aggregate figures discussed here.

### C. Referential Integrity and Data Validation Procedures Across the Jiangsu–Zhejiang Monitoring Network

As explained in the preceding subsection, the confirmation of July readings for municipalities within both Jiangsu and Zhejiang required rigorous cross-validation against provincial registries, a process that necessarily implicated the broader question of database governance and the structural safeguards employed to prevent the introduction of orphaned or unlinked records. A new monthly data record was submitted to the database for processing.
