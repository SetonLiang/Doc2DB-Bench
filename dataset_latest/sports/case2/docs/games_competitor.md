## Implementation

- Establish a mandatory evidence hierarchy for age-related athlete entries, under which roster directives, delegation reports, sport-specific eligibility rules, and birth-year registers are cited in a consistent order, so that the administrative basis for participation is visible rather than inferred.

- Require every Games-era athlete record to distinguish clearly between age derived from roster status, age derived from explicit competition participation, and age derived from aggregate statistical treatment; this would reduce later reconciliation burdens and support more defensible historical interpretation.

- Direct editorial and data teams to annotate exceptional rule environments, including exact-age or minimum-age regimes, in narrative form adjacent to the athlete record, rather than isolating such information in technical appendices that are often overlooked during secondary reuse.

- Introduce longitudinal consistency checks for repeat Olympians, with special review where birth year, age-relative phrasing, and career-sequence descriptions do not align cleanly across editions; where discrepancies remain unresolved, the file should preserve them transparently with source-based qualification.

- Align team-sport and individual-sport reporting templates so that divisional status, senior-level tenure, and roster inclusion criteria are captured as structured contextual fields, particularly in cases where eligibility and experience are jointly relevant to understanding selection and participation.

- Commission a retrospective audit of legacy delegation summaries from earlier editions, including those that relied on aggregate birth-year analyses, so that historically important but methodologically thin age calculations can be supplemented by clearer explanatory notes and modern metadata controls.
