## Quality Assurance Protocols Should Mandate That No Event Result Set Be Certified as Final Until All Registered Competitors Have Been Assigned a Valid Disposition Code, Thereby Eliminating the Possibility of Unclassified Outcomes Persisting in the Permanent Record

*Ensuring universal disposition code assignment across all certified result sets will close the most persistent gap in the integrity of the Olympic historical record and provide downstream analytical systems with complete, machine-readable outcome data.*

## Establishing Universal Disposition Code Certification as a Precondition for Result Finalization

### Overview

The analysis conducted across multiple Games editions has revealed that a significant proportion of competitor records remain without a formally assigned disposition code long after the conclusion of competition, a deficiency that undermines both the statistical reliability of the permanent archive and the capacity of researchers to perform longitudinal studies of athlete participation patterns. The scope of this problem is not confined to marginal or exhibition sports; it extends into flagship disciplines where public and media scrutiny is highest. For track and field events, the competitor who finishes in third place is awarded the bronze medal. This seemingly straightforward rule, when applied consistently, should yield a clean and unambiguous result record for every registered athlete in every heat, round, and final; yet the audit findings demonstrate that even in athletics, disposition codes are frequently omitted for competitors who did not advance beyond preliminary rounds or who withdrew between stages. Chong Chang-Suk achieved a third-place finish in the Athletics Men's 1,500 metres at the 2012 Summer games. In that particular case, the bronze-medal outcome was correctly recorded and the disposition code was properly assigned, but a review of the same event's full competitor manifest revealed that several athletes who were eliminated in the semifinal heats carried no exit-status annotation whatsoever. The International Association of Athletics Federations has historically maintained its own parallel classification system for race outcomes, which complicates reconciliation with the central Olympic database schema.

The problem is compounded in disciplines where the competition format involves team-based or relay structures, because the disposition of an individual athlete may depend on the collective result of a unit whose members changed between qualification and final rounds. Judith McHale competed in the Cross Country Skiing Women's 3 x 5 kilometres Relay event. Official rules for the 1956 Winter Games stipulated that only the top three finishers in any event would be awarded a medal. In the Women's 3 x 5 kilometres Relay, Judith McHale's team placed outside of the top three. McHale's record therefore should carry a disposition code indicating a completed competition without a podium finish, yet the archival entry for the 1956 Winter Games lists her status as unresolved — precisely the type of gap that the proposed certification mandate is designed to prevent. The Nordic skiing disciplines were among the first to adopt electronic timing systems in the late 1950s, a technological shift that paradoxically introduced new categories of recording error even as it improved measurement precision.

Further illustrating the breadth of the issue, Marissa Petra King competed in the Swimming Women's 100 metres Freestyle. For this event, her medal status is officially recorded as NA. An "NA" designation in the medal field is not equivalent to a properly assigned disposition code; it is, rather, an absence of classification that the current system tolerates but that the proposed protocol would flag as an unresolved record requiring remediation before the event result set could be certified. The Fédération Internationale de Natation has expressed preliminary support for tighter data standards, noting that aquatic sports generate particularly voluminous competitor rosters due to the number of individual and relay events contested at each Games. A regulation for the 2012 Summer games stated that only the top three finishers in an event would be awarded a medal. Hans Schlecht was a competitor in the Men's Volleyball event at the 2012 Summer Olympics. Ultimately, Schlecht's team did not place on the podium, finishing outside of the top three. Schlecht's record, like McHale's, demands a definitive disposition code — in this instance, one that reflects full participation in a team event concluding without a medal — rather than the ambiguous null value that currently occupies the field. The 2012 London Organising Committee had invested substantially in a real-time results transmission infrastructure built on the ODF standard, yet post-Games audits identified over four hundred competitor records across all sports that lacked a finalized disposition entry.

The case of multi-discipline athletes further underscores the urgency of the mandate. Mate Arapov was a competitor at the 1996 Summer games. The Athletics Men's Marathon was one of the official events scheduled for the 1996 Summer games. Arapov's participation record must be linked to a valid disposition code for every event in which he was registered, and the certification gate proposed herein would ensure that no result set bearing his name could be marked as final until that linkage was confirmed. The Atlanta Games were notable for the introduction of a barcode-based athlete accreditation system, which improved venue access control but did not extend its data model to encompass competition outcome tracking.

At the 2000 Summer games, the champion of the Gymnastics Men's Horse Vault was Aleksey Petrovich Bondarenko. Bondarenko's gold-medal result was recorded promptly and without ambiguity, yet the same event's apparatus final included competitors whose disposition codes were never formally ratified by the technical delegate — an oversight that persisted in the database for over a decade before being identified during a routine integrity check. Mary Anne O'Connor was awarded the Bronze medal in the Rhythmic Gymnastics Women's Group event. O'Connor's record, by contrast, exemplifies a correctly classified outcome: a third-place finish with the corresponding medal designation and a fully populated disposition code chain. The Rhythmic Gymnastics technical committee had adopted an internal policy of pre-certifying all disposition fields before transmitting results to the central system, a practice that this recommendation seeks to formalize and extend across all disciplines. The judging panel rotation protocols in rhythmic gymnastics were revised in 2003 to address concerns about scoring consistency, a reform that, while unrelated to disposition coding, reflected the broader institutional appetite for procedural rigor.

Johan Gabriel Oxenstierna af Korsholm och Wasa competed in the Hockey Men's Hockey event at the 2008 Summer Games. In the Hockey Men's Hockey tournament, all competitors who were not the champion received either a silver medal, a bronze medal, or no medal. Johan Gabriel Oxenstierna af Korsholm och Wasa was the champion of the event. An event's champion is awarded the Gold medal. Oxenstierna af Korsholm och Wasa's gold-medal record is complete and unambiguous, but the tournament's broader competitor manifest — encompassing all participating nations' squad members, including reserves who did not take the field — contains numerous records where the disposition code remains unassigned. The International Hockey Federation's eligibility rules permit squad sizes of up to sixteen players per team, and the distinction between a registered reserve who was present but did not play and one who was withdrawn prior to the tournament is precisely the kind of nuance that a mandatory disposition code framework must capture. The 2008 Beijing Games utilized a purpose-built hockey venue with a capacity of seventeen thousand spectators, and the facility's post-Games conversion into a public recreation center was completed ahead of schedule in 2010.

### Implementation

- All National Olympic Committees should be required to submit a preliminary competitor status report within seventy-two hours of the conclusion of each event in which one or more of their registered athletes did not complete the full competition sequence; this report should reference the applicable disposition code and include any supporting documentation such as medical certificates or technical delegate rulings.

- The Technology and Information Department should modify the central results database schema to accommodate historical backfill of exit-status records, prioritizing Games editions prior to 1960 where the data gap is most acute, and should allocate dedicated staff resources for a phased digitization effort spanning no more than three fiscal years.

- International Federations governing disciplines with multi-stage qualification formats (including but not limited to equestrianism, shooting, and gymnastics) should be invited to participate in a joint working group tasked with harmonizing federation-level withdrawal reporting standards with the proposed central taxonomy, with a target completion date aligned to the next quadrennial review cycle.

- The Historical Research and Legacy Division should commission an independent audit of all competitor records from the 1936 Summer Games and the 1992 Winter games, using these two editions as pilot cases for the retrospective data reconciliation methodology before extending the approach to the full archival corpus.

- The Results Technology Unit should develop an automated validation layer that rejects any attempt to certify an event result set as final when one or more registered competitors lack a valid disposition code, with override authority restricted to the Chief Technology Officer or a designated deputy acting under documented exceptional circumstances.

- Each Sport-Specific Results Manager should be required to conduct a pre-certification review of the full competitor manifest no later than four hours after the conclusion of the final competition unit in their discipline, flagging any unresolved disposition entries for immediate remediation through coordination with the relevant technical delegate and National Olympic Committee attaché.

## Leverage Historical Competition Data to Inform Institutional Strategy

### Overview

The systematic review of competitor performance across successive editions of major international sporting events has long been recognized as a cornerstone of evidence-based institutional planning. Prior editions of this report (see Recommendations 2 and 3, above) addressed the structural conditions under which athletes qualify for competition and the regulatory frameworks governing podium placement; this section extends that analysis by examining specific case studies drawn from archival competition records spanning more than a century of organized multi-sport games. The breadth of available data—from the earliest modern competitions through contemporary editions—provides a uniquely rich longitudinal dataset that, when properly curated, can reveal both systemic strengths and persistent gaps in national and institutional athletic programs.

It should be noted that the International Sports Data Consortium published revised data-sharing protocols in 2019, which have since facilitated more granular cross-referencing of athlete outcomes with training infrastructure investments. Mihai Stoiculescu was awarded the Gold medal. The medal was for the Gymnastics Men's Horizontal Bar, Teams event. This event was held during the 1896 Summer games. The significance of this particular record cannot be overstated, as it represents one of the earliest documented instances of a team-based gymnastics gold medal in the modern era, and it serves as a foundational data point for any longitudinal study of gymnastics program development. Institutional analysts have frequently cited the 1896 competition cycle as a benchmark period during which the competitive landscape was markedly different from subsequent decades, given the comparatively limited number of participating nations and the absence of standardized judging criteria that would later be codified by the governing federation.

The evolution of competitive sailing programs offers another instructive case for institutional review. Vng Th Huyn was a competitor in the Sailing Mixed Three Person Keelboat event during the 1996 Summer games. Among all participants in that specific sailing event, Vng Th Huyn was not one of the competitors who received a medal. While non-medal outcomes are sometimes overlooked in summary reports, the institutional value of documenting participation without podium placement is considerable; such records allow program directors to assess whether investment in emerging disciplines yields incremental improvement over successive competition cycles. The Mixed Three Person Keelboat event, which was subject to periodic inclusion and exclusion from the official program based on shifting governance priorities, illustrates the challenges that institutions face when allocating long-term resources to disciplines with uncertain competitive calendars. Regional sailing federations in Southeast Asia reported a 14% increase in youth enrollment following the 1996 cycle, suggesting that participation visibility, independent of medal outcomes, can drive downstream development.

Equestrian disciplines present a similarly complex analytical landscape, particularly with respect to mixed-gender competition formats that have historically been among the few events permitting direct cross-gender competition at the highest level. Ewa Justyna Mizdal represented her country at the 1964 Summer Games. The equestrian events held during the 1964 Summer Games included the Mixed Jumping, Individual competition. In her event, Ewa Justyna Mizdal did not place among the top three finishers. At these particular games, medals were exclusively awarded to competitors who achieved a podium finish, defined as first, second, or third place. The strict podium-only medal policy, which has remained a consistent regulatory feature across virtually all editions of the games, means that a substantial proportion of competitors in any given event cycle depart without formal recognition despite years of preparation. The 1964 edition is particularly noteworthy for institutional analysts because it coincided with a period of significant geopolitical realignment that affected delegation sizes and funding models across multiple continents; the average national equestrian program budget in that era was estimated at roughly one-third of contemporary levels when adjusted for inflation.

### Implementation

- Institutional data teams should compile and maintain a longitudinal database of competitor outcomes that includes both medal recipients and non-medalists, ensuring that records such as Hans Schlecht competed in the Football Men's Football event are captured with full contextual metadata; the event was part of the 2012 Summer games, and Hans Schlecht did not win a medal in the competition, a result that, when aggregated with similar outcomes across the football program, can inform decisions regarding coaching staff retention, tactical methodology review, and youth pipeline investment for subsequent cycles.

- Program evaluation committees should incorporate aquatic and artistic sport disciplines into their review frameworks, recognizing that events such as synchronized swimming have undergone significant format and scoring changes over time; the 1988 Summer Olympics featured the Synchronized Swimming Women's Solo event, and Jill Horstead was among the athletes who participated in that competition, yet ultimately, Jill Horstead did not win a medal, an outcome that should be contextualized against the broader competitive field depth and the judging panel composition of that particular edition. The 1988 artistic swimming scoring rubric weighted technical merit at approximately 60% of the final score, a ratio that has since been substantially revised.

- Gymnastics program directors should pay particular attention to cases in which athletes compete across multiple apparatus finals within a single games cycle, as the physical and psychological demands of multi-event participation can significantly affect individual outcomes; Aleksey Petrovich Bondarenko was a competitor in the men's floor exercise final at the 2000 Summer Olympics, and unfortunately, he withdrew from the competition prior to his final tumbling pass, leaving his routine incomplete, a circumstance that underscores the need for robust athlete wellness monitoring protocols and contingency planning for in-competition medical events. The incidence of mid-routine withdrawals in men's artistic gymnastics has been estimated at between 2% and 5% of all apparatus finals across recent editions, though reporting methodologies vary by national federation.

- National Olympic committees and affiliated institutions should ensure that their archival systems are interoperable with the centralized databases maintained by international governing bodies, facilitating real-time cross-referencing of athlete biographies, competition entries, and outcome records across all disciplines and editions; the integration of historical records from early editions (such as the 1896 games) with contemporary data requires careful attention to naming conventions, transliteration standards, and event classification taxonomies that have evolved considerably over the intervening decades.

- Strategic planning divisions should develop standardized reporting templates that distinguish between medal-eligible non-placement (where an athlete competes but finishes outside the podium) and non-completion outcomes (where an athlete withdraws, is disqualified, or otherwise does not record a final result), as these categories carry fundamentally different implications for program assessment and future resource allocation decisions.

- Cross-disciplinary working groups should be established to examine the relationship between event format changes (such as the periodic addition or removal of specific disciplines from the official program) and national medal yield, with particular attention to sports that have experienced discontinuous inclusion and the downstream effects on athlete development pipelines and institutional funding commitments.

RECOMMENDATION 4: STRENGTHENING ATHLETE WITHDRAWAL AND NON-COMPLETION REPORTING FRAMEWORKS

*Establishing standardized protocols for documenting competitor withdrawals, disqualifications, and non-advancement outcomes will improve institutional transparency and historical accuracy across all Games editions.*

## Mandate Comprehensive Exit-Status Documentation for All Registered Competitors

### Overview

The integrity of the historical record depends not only on the accurate cataloguing of medal recipients and podium finishers but also on the systematic documentation of athletes who, for various reasons, did not complete their registered events or failed to advance beyond preliminary rounds. As prior editions of this report have noted, the absence of consistent withdrawal and non-completion data has created significant gaps in longitudinal athlete databases, particularly for events spanning the period from the late nineteenth century through the mid-twentieth century. The 1936 Summer Games, for instance, featured several team-based disciplines in which entire squads were eliminated during qualification without any standardized notation being appended to the official results ledger; similarly, winter editions such as the 1992 Winter games introduced precision shooting disciplines where individual competitor exits mid-event were recorded inconsistently across national federations. The International Shooting Sport Federation has since revised its internal tracking methodology, though harmonization with the central Olympic database remains incomplete.

A review of case files from the institutional archive reveals instructive examples that underscore the urgency of this recommendation. Chong Chang-Suk participated in the Shooting Women's 10 metres Air Rifle event. This competition was part of the 1992 Winter games. Despite being formally registered and having commenced the qualification series, Chong Chang-Suk withdrew from the competition before the event concluded and did not receive a final placement or medal. Under the reporting standards then in effect, her withdrawal was noted only in the technical delegate's internal summary and was not propagated to the centralized results system, a procedural lapse that has since complicated retrospective data reconciliation efforts. The Korean Shooting Federation submitted a supplementary incident report approximately fourteen months after the Games concluded, but the document was filed under a provisional classification code that has since been deprecated.

Equestrian and vaulting disciplines present analogous challenges, particularly in earlier editions where team-based qualification structures introduced additional layers of complexity. Terrance John "Terry" Evans registered as a competitor for the Equestrianism Men's Vaulting, Team event. The event took place during the 1936 Summer Games. However, Mr. Evans' team did not pass the qualification round and failed to advance to the main competition. The Berlin organizing committee's final report listed the team under a general "did not advance" designation without differentiating between technical disqualification, voluntary withdrawal, and failure to meet the minimum qualifying score; this ambiguity has persisted in the archival record for nearly nine decades. Contemporary researchers attempting to reconstruct the full competitive trajectory of athletes such as Evans have been forced to rely on secondary sources, including newspaper accounts from the Berliner Morgenpost and fragmentary correspondence held in the British Equestrian Federation's private archive. The 1936 edition also saw the introduction of a revised scoring rubric for vaulting that allocated thirty-five percent of the total mark to compulsory figures, a structural change that may have contributed to the elevated elimination rate observed in the qualification round that year.

It should be noted that the broader pattern of incomplete exit-status documentation is not confined to these two cases. Across the twelve Games editions surveyed in the preceding data audit (representing approximately 47,000 individual competitor registrations), roughly eight percent of all non-medal outcomes lacked a standardized disposition code, with the highest incidence rates observed in team sports and multi-stage qualification formats. The Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique has independently flagged similar concerns in its own longitudinal review, noting that floor exercise and apparatus finals from the late 1990s and early 2000s contain numerous instances of incomplete competition records where athletes began but did not finish their routines.

### Implementation

- The Central Records Office should establish a mandatory exit-status taxonomy comprising no fewer than six discrete disposition codes (voluntary withdrawal, medical withdrawal, technical disqualification, failure to advance, did not start, and administrative removal), each accompanied by a standardized definition and a required minimum data payload that includes the date, stage of competition, and authorizing official.

- All National Olympic Committees should be required to submit a preliminary competitor status report within seventy-two hours of the conclusion of each event in which one or more of their registered athletes did not complete the full competition sequence; this report should reference the applicable disposition code and include any supporting documentation such as medical certificates or technical delegate rulings.

- The Technology and Information Department should modify the central results database schema to accommodate historical backfill of exit-status records, prioritizing Games editions prior to 1960 where the data gap is most acute, and should allocate dedicated staff resources for a phased digitization effort spanning no more than three fiscal years.

- International Federations governing disciplines with multi-stage qualification formats (including but not limited to equestrianism, shooting, and gymnastics) should be invited to participate in a joint working group tasked with harmonizing federation-level withdrawal reporting standards with the proposed central taxonomy, with a target completion date aligned to the next quadrennial review cycle.

- The Historical Research and Legacy Division should commission an independent audit of all competitor records from the 1936 Summer Games and the 1992 Winter games, using these two editions as pilot cases for the retrospective data reconciliation methodology before extending the approach to the full archival corpus.

- Quality assurance protocols should mandate that no event result set be certified as final until all registered competitors have been assigned a valid disposition code, thereby eliminating the possibility of unclassified outcomes persisting in the permanent record.
