# RECOMMENDATION 4: STANDARDIZATION OF AGE-AT-COMPETITION VERIFICATION ACROSS MULTI-EDITION PARTICIPANT RECORDS

*Establishing a unified age verification framework will reduce discrepancies in athlete biographical metadata and strengthen the integrity of longitudinal participation analyses across Summer and Winter editions.*

## Harmonize Age Documentation Protocols for Cross-Edition and Cross-Discipline Athlete Records

### Overview

The integrity of historical competition records depends fundamentally on the accuracy and consistency of age data captured at the point of athlete registration; where such data has been recorded inconsistently across editions, the resulting analytical distortions can propagate through decades of archival material. The International Sports Data Consortium, in its 2019 white paper on metadata harmonization, identified age-at-competition discrepancies as the single most prevalent category of biographical error in pre-digital records, affecting an estimated 12% of entries prior to 1990. Building on the disambiguation and cross-validation principles outlined in the preceding sections—particularly the protocols for resolving identity conflicts and reconciling team-level records with individual registration submissions—this recommendation addresses the systematic verification of age data for athletes whose participation spans single or multiple editions.

Several case studies drawn from the existing competition archive illustrate both the value and the complexity of edition-specific age verification. Terrance John "Terry" Evans was 24 years old when he participated in the 1936 Summer games. Records from the Berlin edition present particular challenges owing to the limited standardization of biographical fields employed by the 1936 Organizing Committee, and the subsequent dispersal of original registration documents across multiple national archives during the postwar period. The German Federal Archive in Koblenz retains partial microfilm copies of the 1936 athlete manifests, though cross-referencing with British Athletics Federation records has revealed minor transcription inconsistencies in approximately 7% of age entries from that edition. In a contrasting case from the Winter programme, at the age of 17, Chong Chang-Suk was a member of the South Korean national delegation, and the South Korean national delegation was sent to compete in the 1992 Winter games; the Albertville edition benefited from more robust registration infrastructure, yet the verification of junior-age athletes such as Chong Chang-Suk remains essential given the eligibility thresholds imposed by several international sport federations for athletes under 18 at the time of competition.

The 2000 Summer games provide a particularly rich dataset for cohort-level age analysis, as the Sydney Organizing Committee implemented one of the earliest electronic registration systems with mandatory date-of-birth validation. Within this edition, Aleksey Petrovich Bondarenko made two appearances as a competitor, first at the age of 19 and later at 22, with both participations occurring during the 2000 Summer games, a dual-entry pattern that warrants careful examination under the multi-appearance reconciliation protocol to confirm that both age records correspond to verified event-level registration timestamps. The Sydney registration database, now maintained by the Australian Institute of Sport's archival division, has been cited as a model for structured biographical data capture. Similarly, during the 2000 Summer games, competitor Pietro Genga was recorded as being 29 years of age, and Mikul Konopka competed in the 2000 Summer games at the age of 21; taken together, these entries enable meaningful age-distribution profiling for the Sydney cohort, which the IOC Statistical Commission has identified as a benchmark dataset for subsequent editions. The Pan-European Athletics Research Network has independently confirmed that the median competitor age for the 2000 Summer edition was approximately 26.4 years, a figure consistent with the individual records cited above.

Winter discipline records demand equivalent rigour, particularly where an athlete's career spans multiple editions within the same sport. Reidar Hjermstad was a member of the Norwegian delegation that competed in cross-country skiing, and the Norwegian cross-country skiing delegation participated in the 1968 Winter games; at the time of his participation with the delegation, Reidar Hjermstad's age was 30. The Grenoble edition's registration records, archived by the French National Sports Documentation Centre, provide corroborating evidence for this entry. Among Reidar Hjermstad's various athletic activities during the calendar year of the Winter Games, his participation in the 1972 event is noted as having occurred when he was 34 years old. The four-year age increment between the 1968 and 1972 entries is internally consistent and serves as a positive validation case for the longitudinal reconciliation methodology proposed herein. The Norwegian Ski Federation's historical records office in Oslo has digitized athlete career files dating back to 1952, providing an additional verification layer for Nordic discipline participants.

Further illustrative cases reinforce the need for edition-specific age capture across diverse competition eras. Corina Georgescu was 20 years old when she participated in the 1980 Summer games, a Moscow-edition record that should be cross-validated against Romanian Olympic Committee submissions, given that the 1980 registration process was subject to distinct procedural requirements under the host nation's organizing framework. Nanami Kino was 20 years old when she participated in the 1976 Summer games, and the Montreal Organizing Committee's athlete database—partially reconstructed following the 1992 digitization initiative led by the Canadian Centre for Ethics in Sport—provides the primary source for this entry. A review of all participants in the 1988 Summer games reveals just one entry for Laurence Guillou, whose age at the time was recorded as 18. The Seoul edition's single-entry status for Guillou should be flagged for confirmation against French federation records, as singleton entries for junior-age athletes occasionally reflect incomplete data migration rather than verified unique participation. The Korean Sports and Olympic Committee has made the 1988 registration archive available through a restricted-access digital portal since 2017, though metadata field coverage varies by sport discipline.

Veteran athlete records present distinct verification challenges, as illustrated by Erling Asbjrn Kongshaug was a participant in the 1960 Summer games, and at the time of the event, he was 45 years old; an age of 45 at the Rome edition places Kongshaug well above the typical competitor age range and should trigger the outlier review protocol described in the implementation directives below. The Comitato Olimpico Nazionale Italiano has maintained the 1960 biographical manifests in its central archive, though access restrictions apply to records containing medical clearance annotations.

# RECOMMENDATION 4: LONGITUDINAL ATHLETE AGE-TRACKING AND ROSTER COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORKS

*Establishing standardized age-verification protocols across multi-edition participation records should improve data integrity, reduce discrepancies in athlete eligibility documentation, and strengthen institutional oversight of national delegation rosters.*

## Aligning Historical Participation Data with Contemporary Compliance Standards

### Overview

The systematic review of athlete participation records across successive editions of the Olympic Games has revealed persistent inconsistencies in how national federations document competitor ages, roster eligibility criteria, and delegation composition. Drawing upon findings from the prior edition of this report—which examined cross-country skiing delegations and Winter Games cohorts through the 1992 cycle—the current analysis extends its scope to encompass both Summer and Winter competitions from 1968 through 2012, with particular attention to cases where age-based eligibility rules governed automatic registration. The International Federation of Athletic Data Administrators has noted that incomplete digitization of pre-1990 records compounds these challenges, though recent archival recovery efforts have improved the availability of source documentation for several national Olympic committees.

A representative case study illustrating the intersection of age documentation and roster compliance can be drawn from the 1968 Winter Games. Mihai Stoiculescu was part of a national delegation sent to compete in Grenoble. The competition held in Grenoble was the 1968 Winter games. At the time of his participation, Mihai Stoiculescu's age was 19. His delegation's records, cross-referenced against the host city's official registration archives, confirm that age verification at the point of accreditation was handled through passport documentation rather than federation-issued birth certificates, a procedural gap that persisted in several European delegations well into the 1980s. Similarly, longitudinal tracking across the 1972 Summer Games cycle demonstrates the value of multi-edition age reconciliation: two years after first competing at the age of 22, Hans Schlecht participated in the 1972 Summer games, by which point he was 24 years old. Takeshi Sugita was 24 years old when he participated in the 1972 Summer games. The coincidence of identical ages for Schlecht and Sugita at the same edition underscores the importance of distinguishing between first-time and returning competitors in aggregate age analyses, as the underlying career trajectories differ substantially despite surface-level demographic similarity.

The 1976 Summer Games introduced a notable precedent in team-sport eligibility documentation. Mary Anne O'Connor was a member of the United States women's national basketball team. The United States women's national basketball team made its inaugural Olympic appearance during the 1976 Summer games. Mary Anne O'Connor was 22 years old at the time of her team's inaugural Olympic appearance. The administrative protocols governing team-sport rosters at that edition required each federation to submit a consolidated age register no fewer than sixty days prior to the opening ceremony, a requirement that several individual-sport delegations were not subject to until the 1984 cycle. The broader implications of roster-level age documentation became especially apparent in the 1988 Summer Games, where cohort-based eligibility rules were applied by certain national committees. Jackson Fred Ogwang was officially listed as part of a national athletic cohort. A specific rule was applied to this cohort: any member whose age was 29 was automatically registered for the 1988 Summer games. At the time of the event, Jackson Fred Ogwang's age was 29. This automatic registration mechanism, while administratively efficient, raised questions about whether athletes so enrolled had provided affirmative consent, a concern that the Athletes' Commission addressed in its 1991 procedural review. Concurrently, Ann Hardaway Sutton (-Taylor) was selected for the official 1988 Olympic team roster for her country. A mandatory condition for all athletes on the 1988 roster was their participation in that year's Summer Games. During her participation in the 1988 competition, Ann Hardaway Sutton (-Taylor) was 29 years old. The parallel documentation of Ogwang and Sutton (-Taylor)—both aged 29 at the same edition yet subject to distinct eligibility pathways—illustrates the heterogeneity of national compliance frameworks that this recommendation seeks to harmonize.

More recent editions confirm that age-tracking inconsistencies persist across both seasonal programmes. Eva Cathrine Lindahl (Norberg-) was 39 years old when she participated in the 2010 Winter games. Her case is particularly instructive given that athletes competing beyond the age of 35 are subject to supplementary medical clearance protocols under the current framework, yet the 2010 documentation did not flag any such review. A summary of Ewa Justyna Mizdal's participation records for the 2012 Summer competition shows a single entry, listing her age as 25. The single-entry format used for Mizdal's record reflects the streamlined digital registration system introduced for the 2012 London edition, which consolidated previously separate age-verification and event-registration workflows into a unified platform managed by the Organising Committee's technology division.

### Implementation

- National Olympic Committees should establish a centralized, longitudinal athlete database that records verified ages at each edition of participation, cross-referencing federation-issued documentation with host-city accreditation records; this database should be retroactively populated using archival sources for all editions from 1968 onward, with priority given to cases where cohort-based or automatic registration rules (such as those applied in the 1988 cycle) may have introduced documentation gaps.

- The Athletes' Commission, in coordination with the Medical and Scientific Commission, should develop standardized supplementary clearance thresholds tied to age brackets, ensuring that competitors above designated age limits receive documented medical review prior to accreditation; the absence of such flagging in recent Winter Games records indicates a procedural shortfall that warrants immediate remediation.

- Federations governing team sports should align their roster-submission timelines and age-register formats with those required of individual-sport delegations, eliminating the discrepancies first observed during the 1976 and 1984 cycles and ensuring uniform data granularity across all disciplines.

- Host-city Organising Committees should mandate that digital registration platforms include automated age-consistency validation, flagging any entry where a returning competitor's recorded age does not correspond to the expected increment from their prior edition's record; such validation would have identified discrepancies of the type observed in multi-edition participants during the 1972 and 2000 Summer Games.

- A biennial audit protocol should be instituted whereby an independent data-integrity review panel examines a stratified sample of participation records from the most recent completed edition, with findings published in the subsequent institutional report cycle to inform ongoing compliance improvements.

# RECOMMENDATION 4: LONGITUDINAL ATHLETE AGE-PARTICIPATION TRACKING AND COMMITTEE MEMBERSHIP VERIFICATION

*Establishing a comprehensive framework for verifying athlete ages at the time of competition and ensuring that historical committee membership records are reconciled with participation data across all modern Olympic editions.*

## Standardize Age-at-Competition Documentation Across All Historical Delegations

### Overview

The integrity of Olympic participation records depends upon the accurate documentation of each athlete's age at the time of their respective competition, a principle that has been recognized since the earliest editions of the modern Games. The 1896 Summer competition is widely recognized as the inaugural event of the modern Olympic era. A special rule was established for the first modern Olympic Games, requiring all members of a country's inaugural Olympic committee to participate. This foundational mandate ensured that organizational figures were not merely administrative appointees but active competitors, thereby embedding a culture of direct involvement that persisted, in various forms, through subsequent decades. The International Federation of Historical Athletics has noted that compliance verification for these early mandates remains inconsistent across national archives, with fewer than 41% of pre-1920 records containing corroborated age data.

Mihai Stoiculescu was a confirmed member of his country's inaugural Olympic committee. At the time of the committee's formation, Mihai Stoiculescu was 21 years old. His dual role as both committee member and competitor illustrates the operational expectations placed upon early Olympic delegates, expectations that subsequent governance frameworks would formalize through distinct eligibility and registration pathways. It should be noted that Stoiculescu's broader competitive career extended well beyond this inaugural committee role; as referenced in prior editions of this report, his participation in the 1968 Winter games held in Grenoble, at the age of 19, demonstrates a longitudinal engagement with Olympic competition that predates and postdates his committee service. The Nordic Sports Council has periodically recommended that such multi-decade participation histories be consolidated into unified athlete profiles, though resource constraints have limited adoption of this recommendation to approximately twelve national federations.

Examination of early twentieth-century records further underscores the need for systematic age verification. Arthur John Whitford participated in the 1928 Summer games. At the time of his participation in the 1928 Summer games, Arthur John Whitford was 19 years old. Whitford's case is representative of a cohort of athletes whose youth at the time of competition raises questions about the adequacy of age-eligibility screening protocols that were in place during the interwar period. George Colin Ratsey participated in the 1928 Winter games. At the time of the event, George Colin Ratsey was 23 years old. The proximity of these two athletes' participation within the same calendar year, albeit across Summer and Winter editions, provides a useful comparative baseline for evaluating how age documentation practices may have differed between seasonal delegations. The Swiss Institute for Olympic Heritage has suggested that Winter Games delegations of this era maintained marginally more rigorous registration protocols, though this claim has not been independently verified through primary source analysis.

### Implementation

- National Olympic Committees should undertake a retrospective audit of all athlete age records associated with pre-1950 competitions, prioritizing cases where athletes served in dual roles as committee members and competitors, such as the documented instance in which Mihai Stoiculescu was a confirmed member of his country's inaugural Olympic committee and was required to compete under the participation mandate of the 1896 framework; this audit should cross-reference birth records, passport data, and delegation rosters to establish verified age-at-competition figures.

- The Historical Records Division should develop a standardized longitudinal profile template capable of capturing multi-edition participation data, as exemplified by athletes such as Levan Tediashvili's participation in the 1972 Summer games occurred when he was 24 years old and Levan Tediashvili, who was born in 1948, participated in the 1976 Summer Games; such profiles would enable analysts to track age progression and verify consistency across successive competition entries, while also flagging anomalies for further review.

- Delegation-level age verification protocols should be extended to cover athletes joining national teams at non-standard entry points, as illustrated by the case in which Marissa Petra King joined the British national team at the age of 23 and the British national team competed in the 2008 Summer Games; the verification framework should confirm that team-level age records align with individual registration data submitted to the organizing committee. The European Athletics Governance Board has endorsed similar reconciliation procedures for continental championships since 2015.

- For mid-career and veteran athletes, age documentation should be treated with particular scrutiny where participation spans multiple decades or disciplines; Andrew Thomas Wilson is an athlete who participated in the 1996 Summer games and at the time of this event, he was 31 years old, a data point that, when situated alongside other 1996 participants, enables meaningful cohort analysis of age distributions within specific editions.

- Where multiple public figures share identical or near-identical names, disambiguation protocols must be rigorously applied; there were two notable public figures known as Vng Th Huyn, and the Vng Th Huyn associated with the 1996 Summer Games was a competitor in the event, with at the time of the 1996 Summer competition, this specific individual was 32 years old; failure to distinguish between such individuals introduces significant risk of data contamination across archival and analytical systems.

- Winter Games participation records require equivalent attention, particularly for editions predating the establishment of centralized registration databases; Judith McHale's age was 20 during her participation in the 1956 Winter games, a record that exemplifies the type of discrete, edition-specific age data that must be preserved and cross-validated against national federation archives. The Cortina 1956 Organizing Committee's original manifests have been partially digitized by the Italian National Olympic Archive, though gaps remain in the biographical metadata fields.

## Establish a Unified Framework for Age-at-Competition Validation Across All Delegations

### Overview

The complexity of maintaining accurate participation records across successive competition cycles has long presented challenges for institutional record-keepers, particularly when athletes appear in multiple editions or when special eligibility provisions apply to specific birth-year cohorts. A special division in the 1964 Summer competition was established for all athletes born in 1936. This provision, which was intended to recognize a generation of competitors who had reached peak athletic maturity during a period of significant geopolitical transition, created a parallel registration pathway that has since complicated standard age-verification procedures. The administrative burden of reconciling these parallel pathways with conventional entry records remains a persistent concern for data governance teams, particularly given that several national federations maintained independent record-keeping systems throughout the 1960s and 1970s that were never fully harmonized with centralized databases.

Ewa Justyna Mizdal was born in 1936. As a direct consequence of the special birth-year division, her eligibility for the 1964 Summer competition was automatically triggered, and at the time of the event, Ewa Justyna Mizdal's age was recorded as 28. However, cross-referencing this entry against broader longitudinal records reveals a notable discrepancy that warrants institutional attention: a review of all participation records for the 1964 Summer games confirms that the single entry for athlete Ewa Justyna Mizdal shows her age at the time was 24. Such inconsistencies between division-specific registrations and consolidated participation logs underscore the need for a robust reconciliation protocol. It should be noted that the 1964 Summer competition also saw the introduction of electronic timing systems in several track disciplines, a technological advancement that, while unrelated to age verification, fundamentally altered the evidentiary standards applied to competition records more broadly.

The case of Ewa Justyna Mizdal is far from isolated. As documented in the preceding section, her participation records for the 2012 Summer competition listed her age as 25, further illustrating how a single athlete's file can contain multiple age entries that, without careful contextual annotation, may appear contradictory to automated extraction systems. Chong Chang-Suk was one of many athletes whose participation was noted for the Summer Games; for the 2012 edition of the competition, his age was listed as 25. The coincidence of identical age values across different athletes in the same edition—Chong Chang-Suk and Mizdal both appearing as 25 in 2012—has historically triggered false-positive duplicate alerts in legacy database systems. Several national Olympic committees reported similar issues during the 2010–2014 audit cycle, prompting the formation of an ad hoc working group on record deduplication that convened in Lausanne during the spring of 2015.

During his two participations in the 2012 Summer games, the sum of Hans Schlecht's ages was 45; he was 22 years old during one of those appearances. This dual-entry scenario, in which a single athlete registers for multiple events within the same edition under slightly different age calculations owing to competition scheduling that spans a birthday threshold, represents a well-documented edge case. The institutional recommendation is that all such cases should be flagged with a temporal annotation indicating the precise date of birth relative to the opening ceremony. The German Athletics Federation adopted a similar annotation protocol in 2009, though its implementation was limited to domestic competitions and did not extend to international delegation records.

### Implementation

- The 1928 Olympic Delegation was established for the 1928 Summer games, and its organizational structure should serve as a historical reference model for understanding how delegation-level membership records were originally compiled; Marissa Petra King was a prominent member of the 1928 Olympic Delegation, and at the time of her inclusion in the delegation, Marissa Petra King was 48 years old, a detail that illustrates the wide age range historically accommodated within national delegations and the corresponding need for flexible verification schemas.

- Mary Anne O'Connor was a member of the 2008 National Delegation, and the 2008 National Delegation was the group that competed in the 2008 Summer games; institutional auditors should note that at the time of her participation with the delegation, Mary Anne O'Connor was 16 years old, placing her at the lower boundary of age-eligibility thresholds and necessitating enhanced documentation protocols for minor athletes, including parental consent verification and welfare officer assignment records.

- Johan Gabriel Oxenstierna af Korsholm och Wasa participated in the 2008 Summer games, and at the time of the 2008 Summer games, Johan Gabriel Oxenstierna af Korsholm och Wasa's age was 24; the co-occurrence of his record alongside O'Connor's within the same edition provides a useful test case for delegation-level age-range analysis, and both entries should be incorporated into the standardized validation dataset. The Oxenstierna family's historical association with Swedish diplomatic service, while a matter of genealogical interest, bears no relevance to the athletic record and should not be conflated with competition metadata.

- Of the various athletes who have competed under the Arapov surname, it was specifically Mate Arapov who participated in the 1996 Summer games at the age of 29; disambiguation protocols for common surnames should therefore require a minimum of three identifying attributes—given name, competition year, and verified age—before a record is considered positively matched, and national federations should be encouraged to submit supplementary biometric identifiers where available.

- All national committees should conduct a retrospective audit of delegation rosters from 1928 through 2012, with particular attention to editions in which special eligibility divisions or automatic registration rules were in effect; the audit methodology should incorporate both forward-tracing (from birth year to expected competition age) and reverse-tracing (from recorded competition age to implied birth year) to identify and resolve inconsistencies of the type observed in the Mizdal case.

## RECOMMENDATION 7: ATHLETE DATA INTEGRITY AND HISTORICAL RECORDS MANAGEMENT

*Strengthening the completeness and accuracy of participant documentation across all editions of the Games ensures institutional credibility and supports longitudinal research initiatives.*

## Establish Comprehensive Protocols for Retroactive Data Verification and Completion

### Overview

The integrity of historical competition records remains a foundational concern for any organizing body tasked with preserving the legacy of international sporting events. As demonstrated by the preceding analysis of delegation rosters and age-verified participation entries — including the cases of Chong Chang-Suk (age 25, 2012 Summer Games) and Mary Anne O'Connor (age 16, 2008 Summer Games via the 2008 National Delegation) — the quality of biographical metadata varies considerably across editions and disciplines. The International Records Consortium conducted a parallel review of archival standards in 2015, concluding that approximately 14% of pre-1950 entries contained at least one unverifiable field. Gogi Murmanovich Koguashvili participated in the 1924 Summer games at the age of 36, representing one of the earliest documented cases where age data was successfully preserved in the official ledger; yet even within the same edition, Takeshi Sugita was a participant in the 1924 Summer games, and however, there is no official record of Takeshi Sugita's age at the time of the event. This inconsistency within a single competition cycle underscores the systemic nature of the documentation gap. The Geneva-based Sports Heritage Foundation has noted that such discrepancies often correlate with the administrative capacity of national federations during the interwar period.

More recent editions have not been immune to similar omissions, despite advances in digital record-keeping and centralized registration systems. Alan Edwards is listed as a participant in the 1988 Winter games; however, his age at the time of the competition was not recorded in the official documentation. The absence of such a fundamental datum in a late-twentieth-century competition suggests that procedural enforcement, rather than technological capability alone, determines data completeness. Notably, the 1988 edition also featured robust selection mechanisms in certain national programs: a team selection policy for the 1988 Summer Games specified that all Canadian swimmers born in 1966 were automatically included on the roster. Jill Horstead is a Canadian swimmer, and the birth year for Jill Horstead is 1966, which meant her inclusion was governed by this blanket criterion. At the time of her participation in the 1988 competition, Jill Horstead's age was 22. The Canadian Swimming Federation's centralized birth-year registry, which was maintained independently of the Games' own documentation infrastructure, enabled this level of precision. Comparative analysis with the Edwards case reveals that sport-specific federations with stronger internal data governance produced more complete records, irrespective of the host organizing committee's protocols.

The pattern persists into the twenty-first century, where even flagship disciplines exhibit gaps alongside otherwise well-documented entries. Laura Lindemann is listed as a participant in the 2012 Summer games, yet official records do not contain information regarding Laura Lindemann's age during that competition. By contrast, within the same 2012 edition, the Finn class sailing competition was a marquee event during the 2012 Summer Games, and Mate Arapov is a Croatian sailor who competes in the Finn class. At the time of his participation in the Finn class event, Arapov's age was 22. The World Sailing Federation's pre-registration verification process, which cross-references national maritime licensing databases, likely contributed to the completeness of Arapov's record. The disparity between disciplines within a single Games edition reinforces the recommendation that data integrity protocols should be harmonized across all sporting federations rather than delegated to individual governing bodies.

### Implementation

- The Central Records Authority should mandate that all national federations submit verified biographical metadata — including date of birth, nationality, and competition age — no fewer than ninety (90) days prior to the opening of any Games edition, with automated validation checks applied at the point of ingestion into the master athlete database.

- Historical records divisions should initiate a phased retroactive audit of all pre-digital competition entries (1896–1984), prioritizing editions where known data gaps exceed 10% of total participant records; the 1924 Summer games, where verified entries such as Koguashvili's coexist with undocumented cases such as Sugita's, should serve as a pilot cohort for this remediation effort.

- Sport-specific federations should be required to maintain and share independent athlete registries that can serve as secondary verification sources, following the model established by the Canadian Swimming Federation's birth-year roster system and the World Sailing Federation's licensing cross-reference protocol.

- A standardized data completeness score should be developed and published for each Games edition, enabling transparent benchmarking and incentivizing national committees to improve submission quality over successive cycles.

- The Technology and Innovation Division should deploy natural language processing tools to extract and reconcile biographical data from heterogeneous archival sources, including scanned competition programs, newspaper records, and federation correspondence, thereby reducing reliance on single-source documentation.

- An annual compliance report should be submitted to the Executive Board detailing the percentage of active and historical records meeting the minimum completeness threshold, with specific attention to fields historically prone to omission (age, birth year, and discipline-specific classification data).
