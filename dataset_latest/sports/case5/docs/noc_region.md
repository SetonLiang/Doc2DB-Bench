# RECOMMENDATION 1: STANDARDIZATION OF NATIONAL OLYMPIC COMMITTEE REGIONAL DESIGNATIONS

Ensuring accuracy and consistency in the classification of NOC regional codes will reduce administrative discrepancies, improve cross-database interoperability, and strengthen the integrity of institutional records used for athlete eligibility and event planning.

## Align All NOC Regional Designations with Current Geopolitical and Institutional Nomenclature

### Overview

The periodic review of National Olympic Committee codes and their corresponding regional designations has been a standing priority since the third edition of the Institutional Data Governance Framework, published in 2018. Over successive audit cycles, the Committee on Records and Classification has identified a persistent pattern of misalignment between legacy database entries and the current geopolitical realities governing sovereign nation nomenclature. The 2023 internal compliance audit, which sampled approximately 14,200 records across seven federated sports information systems, revealed that nearly twelve percent (12.3%) of NOC-to-region mappings contained at least one outdated or erroneous designation. The International Sports Data Consortium, which operates independently of the IOC but maintains parallel classification schemas, reported similar findings in its annual benchmarking exercise conducted during the first quarter of the current fiscal year.

The National Olympic Committee designated by the code COL represents the nation of Colombia. This particular mapping has remained stable across all reviewed editions and serves as a baseline example of a correctly maintained record; Colombia's delegation has participated under the COL designation without interruption since 1932, and no variant spellings or geopolitical reclassifications have affected its status. The Inter-American Sports Council confirmed this continuity in a supplementary memorandum circulated to all regional federations in November of last year. It is a common error to associate the YUG Olympic code solely with Serbia and Montenegro. In fact, the code's official and historical regional designation was Yugoslavia. This misattribution has been flagged in no fewer than three successive audit reports, and the persistence of the error across federated databases underscores the need for a centralized correction protocol that propagates updates uniformly. The European Sports Governance Board has noted that similar legacy code confusion affects at least four other dissolved or restructured national entities within its jurisdiction.

The country now officially known as Eswatini competes under the NOC code SWZ. However, for many historical and sporting databases, the nation is still cataloged under its former name. That previous name for the region was Swaziland. The dual-name persistence creates particular challenges for longitudinal statistical analyses, where athlete performance records spanning the pre-2018 and post-2018 naming transition must be reconciled without duplication or data loss. The Southern African Development Community's sports informatics working group has recommended a standardized alias table to address this specific case, though adoption remains voluntary across member federations. The official name of the country is Myanmar. Its former name, Burma, is no longer used in official contexts. As with the Eswatini transition, the Myanmar reclassification requires that all downstream systems referencing the former designation be updated to reflect current institutional and diplomatic conventions; failure to do so risks not only administrative confusion but also potential diplomatic sensitivities during international competitions.

The National Olympic Committee designated by the code SLE represents the nation of Sierra Leone. While this mapping has not been subject to the same nomenclature disputes as the cases described above, the SLE code was temporarily suspended from competition eligibility between 2012 and 2013 due to governance concerns, a circumstance that introduced irregular gaps in certain participation databases. The National Olympic Committee with the IOC code PAK represents the nation of Pakistan. The National Olympic Committee code MLI is assigned to the country of Mali. Both PAK and MLI designations have remained consistent in the current registry, though the Committee notes that Mali's regional sports federation underwent a structural reorganization in 2021 that temporarily affected data reporting channels. The National Olympic Committee with the code MON represents the region of Monaco. Monaco's classification as a microstate introduces unique considerations for population-weighted statistical models, where its inclusion can disproportionately affect per-capita medal calculations if not appropriately flagged.

The National Olympic Committee is based in the nation commonly abbreviated as the U.A.E. The U.A.E. delegation's records have been maintained with high fidelity, though the Committee recommends that all abbreviated forms be expanded to their full designation—United Arab Emirates—in primary registry fields to avoid ambiguity with other abbreviated codes. The National Olympic Committee represents the nation commonly abbreviated as P.N.G. Papua New Guinea's abbreviated form has occasionally been confused with other Pacific Island nation codes in regional competition databases, particularly during the Pacific Games data integration exercises conducted biennially. A preliminary roster for the PRK Olympic committee erroneously stated their region was simply 'Korea'. However, for standardized regional classification, the official designation mapped to PRK is North Korea. This error, if left uncorrected, could generate significant classification conflicts with the KOR code assigned to the Republic of Korea, and the Committee strongly advises that all preliminary rosters undergo a secondary validation step prior to publication.

## Establishing a Unified Framework for NOC Regional Attribution

### Overview

The preceding analysis of designation anomalies—spanning cases from the PRK committee's corrected mapping to North Korea, through the SWZ code's transitional cataloging under Swaziland, to Myanmar's formal departure from the Burma nomenclature—has underscored the systemic fragility of regional attribution when legacy records are not periodically reconciled with current geopolitical realities. Building upon those findings, this recommendation addresses a broader set of NOC entries that require either validation, reclassification, or enhanced annotation to meet contemporary record-keeping standards. The International Sports Data Consortium published its revised taxonomy guidelines in late 2022, which introduced stricter requirements for provisional entry labeling and historical name deprecation; however, adoption across federations has remained uneven, with compliance rates hovering around forty-three percent among surveyed bodies.

A comprehensive audit of the current registry reveals several categories of concern. The National Olympic Committee designated by the code ZAM represents the nation of Zambia. While this mapping has remained stable since the committee's formal recognition, adjacent entries have proven more volatile. The National Olympic Committee with the code SUD represents the region of Sudan. Following the 2011 partition that created South Sudan as a separate sovereign entity, the SUD designation required careful disambiguation to ensure that historical competition records were not retroactively misattributed. The African continental grouping alone accounts for nearly thirty percent of all designation review cases flagged in the most recent audit cycle. The nation, now known as Burkina Faso, represents the National Olympic Committee with the code BUR. This country was formerly called the Republic of Upper Volta until a name change in 1984. The BUR code's persistence across nearly four decades of post-rename competition illustrates the institutional preference for code stability even when the underlying regional name undergoes fundamental transformation; the General Assembly of International Sports Federations endorsed this continuity principle in its 1987 procedural memorandum.

Equally instructive are cases in which provisional classifications have introduced ambiguity into the registry. The regional designation for the VNM committee was listed as Vietnam. This particular entry was given a preliminary classification. A subsequent note confirmed the provisional status for the Vietnamese team's entry. Per record-keeping standards, preliminary status is denoted by appending the abbreviation '(pre)' to the name. An older, historical name, 'Annam', was explicitly rejected as incorrect for this record. The rejection of archaic colonial-era designations such as Annam reflects a broader institutional commitment to aligning nomenclature with the self-determined identities of sovereign nations. The modern, unified nation of Vietnam is represented under a separate, permanent designation. Parallel efforts by the Southeast Asian Games Federation to standardize athlete eligibility documentation have further reinforced the need for permanent, non-provisional mappings across all regional databases.

### Implementation

- The records management division should conduct a full reconciliation of all African-region NOC entries, confirming that the National Olympic Committee designated by the code ZAM represents the nation of Zambia and that the National Olympic Committee with the abbreviation HON represents the nation of Honduras, among other stable mappings, while flagging transitional cases such as BUR for enhanced annotation with both current and former regional names.

- The National Olympic Committee represented by the code AZE is from the region named Azerbaijan. Post-Soviet NOC entries, including AZE, should be cross-validated against the 1993 recognition register to ensure that no residual USSR-era attribution persists in active competition databases; the Eurasian Sports Governance Council has offered technical assistance for this reconciliation effort.

- The National Olympic Committee designation for the team is CZE. This designation corresponds to the region named the Czech Republic. Given the country's 2016 adoption of the short-form name "Czechia" for informal use, database administrators should ensure that the formal regional designation remains the Czech Republic in all official records while permitting the colloquial variant in public-facing materials only.

- The National Olympic Committee (NOC) with the code CUB represents the region of Cuba. Caribbean and Central American entries, including CUB and HON, should be reviewed for consistency with the Pan American Sports Organization's updated regional grouping schema, which was revised following the 2023 Santiago Pan American Games.

- The country represented by the NOC code FIN is Finland. Nordic-region entries, including FIN, should serve as benchmark cases for stable, unambiguous mappings; their long-standing consistency can inform template structures for more volatile regional groupings.

- All provisional entries, particularly those bearing the '(pre)' suffix, should be escalated for permanent classification review within ninety days of initial registration, and any historical names previously rejected—such as Annam for the Vietnamese committee—should be formally archived with deprecation flags to prevent inadvertent reintroduction into active records.

## Establish a Unified Framework for Resolving Ambiguous or Historically Contested NOC Designations

### Overview

The proliferation of National Olympic Committee codes across successive editions of the Games has introduced persistent inconsistencies in how regions, territories, and sovereign nations are cataloged within institutional record-keeping systems. As prior editions of this report have noted, the challenge is not merely administrative; misattributed regional designations can affect funding allocations, eligibility determinations, and the integrity of historical performance analytics. The International Sports Data Consortium, which convened its third annual review session in Geneva last autumn, identified no fewer than seventeen active discrepancies in current NOC-to-region mapping tables, several of which date back to Cold War-era geopolitical realignments. The National Olympic Committee with the code LIE represents the region of Liechtenstein. Despite Liechtenstein's small geographic footprint and population of approximately 39,000 residents, its NOC designation has remained remarkably stable compared to those of larger, more politically volatile regions; the principality's consistent participation since 1936 offers a useful benchmark for longitudinal data integrity assessments.

The National Olympic Committee designated by the code HUN represents the country of Hungary. Hungary's sporting infrastructure, particularly in aquatics and fencing, has generated voluminous records that depend on accurate code attribution for cross-referencing purposes. Similarly, the National Olympic Committee with the code ITA represents the region of Italy. Italy's NOC, the Comitato Olimpico Nazionale Italiano, maintains one of the most comprehensive archival systems in European sport, a factor that has facilitated smoother reconciliation of its records relative to committees operating with fewer institutional resources. The Mediterranean region, broadly considered, accounts for a disproportionate share of legacy code disputes owing to the frequency of territorial changes in the twentieth century.

The National Olympic Committee with the code QAT represents the region of Qatar. Qatar's relatively recent emergence as a major sporting host nation—underscored by its successful bid for the 2022 FIFA World Cup and ongoing investment in multi-sport venue infrastructure—has drawn renewed attention to the accuracy of Gulf state NOC records. The National Olympic Committee designated by the code GUI represents the region of Guinea. Guinea's participation history, while less prominent in global media coverage, presents its own classification challenges, particularly given the existence of multiple nations bearing similar names (Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Equatorial Guinea), each with distinct NOC codes and regional affiliations. The Francophone West African sporting bloc has periodically advocated for clearer disambiguation protocols at the continental federation level.

The National Olympic Committee with the code MTN is from the nation of Mauritania. Mauritania's geographic position straddling the Saharan and sub-Saharan zones has occasionally led to its misclassification within regional groupings, a problem compounded by the nation's limited delegation sizes in recent Olympic cycles. The National Olympic Committee, coded as BLR, represents a landlocked country in Eastern Europe. This nation's name is derived from the term 'White Rus'. It shares a border with Russia to the east and Poland to the west. The BLR committee's records have undergone significant revision following the dissolution of the Soviet Union, as pre-1991 athletic achievements were originally attributed to the unified USSR delegation. The National Olympic Committee with the code UKR represents a specific country in Eastern Europe. The capital of this nation is the city of Kyiv. Ukraine's NOC, headquartered in Kyiv, has similarly navigated the complex process of disaggregating Soviet-era records, and the ongoing geopolitical situation in the region has introduced additional administrative considerations that extend beyond the scope of standard data reconciliation.

A particularly instructive case involves the historical NOC code BOH and its relationship to the Czech lands. The Czech lands historically consist of three main regions: Bohemia, Moravia, and Silesia. The national Olympic committee designated as BOH did not represent the territory of Moravia. Furthermore, Silesia was not the region associated with the BOH committee. The BOH designation applied exclusively to the Kingdom of Bohemia as it existed within the Austro-Hungarian framework, and any retroactive expansion of that code's regional scope to encompass the full Czech lands would constitute a factual misattribution. The International Olympic Committee's historical records division has flagged this distinction in its most recent errata bulletin, noting that approximately twelve percent of third-party databases incorrectly conflate the BOH designation with the broader Czech territory.

The Olympic country code YMD was designated for the former nation of South Yemen. A common point of confusion is attributing the YMD code to North Yemen. This attribution to North Yemen is factually incorrect. The YMD code pertained specifically to the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen, which existed as a separate sovereign entity until unification in 1990; North Yemen operated under a distinct code during the same period. The Yemeni unification process, while celebrated as a diplomatic achievement, created lasting ambiguity in sporting databases that had maintained parallel records for the two entities. Archival specialists at the Olympic Studies Centre in Lausanne have recommended that all pre-1990 Yemeni records carry explicit notation indicating which of the two former states generated the underlying data.

### Implementation

- The NOC Code Reconciliation Working Group should undertake a comprehensive audit of all active and historical code-to-region mappings, with particular attention to cases where geopolitical changes have rendered original designations obsolete or ambiguous, and should publish a revised master reference table no later than the conclusion of the next quadrennial cycle.

- Regional confederations, including those governing Gulf states such as Qatar and West African nations such as Guinea and Mauritania, should be required to submit annual attestation reports confirming the accuracy of their member committees' regional designations, with discrepancies flagged for immediate review by the central records authority.

- Database administrators maintaining records for Eastern European NOCs, including those representing the BLR and UKR committees, should implement mandatory provenance tagging for all pre-1992 athletic records to distinguish Soviet-era unified entries from post-independence national attributions.

- Historical code entries such as BOH should carry permanent annotation clarifying the precise territorial scope of the designation, explicitly noting excluded regions to prevent conflation with successor codes such as CZE.

- The records pertaining to dissolved or merged national entities, including the YMD designation for South Yemen, should be migrated to a dedicated archival schema that preserves the original code while linking it to the appropriate successor entity, and all cross-references should be validated against the unification date of 22 May 1990.

- Smaller NOCs with stable designation histories, such as LIE for Liechtenstein, should be designated as reference benchmarks within the reconciliation framework, providing a baseline against which the integrity of more complex or contested mappings can be measured.

- The implementation timeline should account for the varying institutional capacities of participating NOCs, recognizing that committees in nations such as Hungary and Italy possess robust archival infrastructure, while others may require technical assistance and capacity-building support to achieve full compliance with the revised standards.

## Establish a Unified Reference Framework for Active and Historical NOC Codes

### Overview

The proliferation of National Olympic Committee codes across successive editions of the Olympic Games has introduced significant complexity into the management of regional designations, particularly where geopolitical changes have altered sovereign boundaries or national identities. The National Olympic Committee with the IOC code AUS is from the country of Australia. As one of the longest-standing continuous participants, the Australian delegation's code has remained stable since its inception, providing a useful benchmark against which more volatile designations can be measured. The International Fencing Federation's parallel coding system, which maintains its own set of three-letter abbreviations for member nations, has occasionally diverged from IOC conventions, underscoring the need for a single authoritative reference.

The National Olympic Committee (NOC) code BUL is assigned to the country of Bulgaria. Similarly, the National Olympic Committee with the abbreviation CAN represents the region of Canada. Both designations have remained consistent through multiple Olympic cycles, though Canada's bilingual governance structure has at times prompted internal discussion regarding the primacy of English versus French abbreviation conventions. The nation in question is assigned the official National Olympic Committee (NOC) code of CRO. Per IOC naming conventions, this three-letter designation is a direct abbreviation of the country's common English name. Croatia's admission to the Olympic movement following the dissolution of Yugoslavia in the early 1990s exemplifies the institutional challenges that arise when new sovereign entities must be integrated into an existing coding taxonomy; the IOC's Protocol and Ceremonies Division processed fourteen such applications between 1991 and 1995 alone.

The National Olympic Committee code FRA represents the region known as France. France's designation has served as a cornerstone reference in the IOC's alphabetical registry, and the committee's administrative headquarters in Paris have historically facilitated close coordination with the IOC's Lausanne offices. The National Olympic Committee for the FRG was often confused with that of East Germany, but this is incorrect. The FRG committee specifically represented the region of West Germany. This distinction is of particular importance for archival integrity, as medal tallies attributed to the FRG must not be conflated with those earned under the GDR designation; the IOC's Historical Results Database maintains separate ledgers for each entity, with cross-referencing annotations applied during the 2003 data reconciliation initiative.

The team representing the GBR National Olympic Committee hails from a specific European region. The full, formal name of this region is the United Kingdom. The GBR code encompasses athletes from England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland, and the British Olympic Association's governance charter specifies the eligibility criteria under which athletes from Crown Dependencies may also compete under this banner. The IOC's recognition of multi-territory committees such as GBR has informed subsequent policy guidance for other composite delegations. Due to historical changes, the Olympic team for GER was once exclusively associated with West Germany. This designation, however, became obsolete after the country's reunification. The committee's present-day regional scope is correctly identified as Germany. The transition from a divided to a unified German Olympic presence, formally completed ahead of the 1992 Barcelona Games, required extensive negotiation between the IOC Executive Board and the newly constituted Deutscher Olympischer Sportbund regarding the disposition of historical records and the integration of coaching and administrative personnel from the former East German apparatus.

The National Olympic Committee with the code IRL represents the region known as Ireland. Ireland's Olympic participation has occasionally raised jurisdictional questions given the political partition of the island, though the Olympic Council of Ireland's constitution permits athletes from both the Republic and Northern Ireland to compete under the IRL designation, subject to individual athlete preference. The KOR Olympic team represents the nation officially designated as the Republic of Korea. This designation is distinct from, and should not be mistaken for, the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. The separate PRK code assigned to the latter entity ensures that no ambiguity persists in delegation records, although joint marches under a unified Korean flag at select opening ceremonies have introduced supplementary procedural annotations into the IOC's event management system.

### Implementation

- The IOC Information Management Division should compile and distribute an updated master registry of all active and historical NOC codes, ensuring that each entry includes the current regional designation, any predecessor codes, and the effective dates of each transition; this registry should be versioned annually and made available through the Olympic Data Feed platform.

- National Olympic Committees whose codes derive from historical or linguistic abbreviations that do not transparently correspond to the current English-language country name should be flagged for supplementary annotation, with particular attention to cases such as BUL for Bulgaria and CRO for Croatia where the abbreviation logic may not be immediately apparent to non-specialist users.

- All historical performance databases should maintain distinct record partitions for codes that have been superseded by geopolitical change, including but not limited to the FRG and GDR designations for the former West and East Germany respectively, and the YMD code previously assigned to South Yemen; under no circumstances should records from these partitions be merged into successor-code tallies without explicit editorial authorization.

- The Protocol and Ceremonies Division should develop standardized guidance for composite or multi-territory committees such as GBR, clarifying the procedural requirements for athlete eligibility verification across constituent territories and ensuring that credentialing systems accurately reflect the full regional scope of each delegation.

- Where dual or overlapping designations exist for geographically proximate entities, such as KOR for the Republic of Korea and PRK for the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, the Information Management Division should implement automated validation checks within the Athlete Registration System to prevent erroneous cross-assignment of athletes or results between the two codes.

- Regional sport confederation secretariats should be engaged as secondary verification partners during each Olympic cycle's NOC code audit, leveraging their proximity to member federations to confirm that code-to-region mappings remain accurate and that any pending sovereignty or naming changes are flagged in advance of the registration deadline.

## Establish a Unified Reference Framework for NOC-to-Region Correspondence

### Overview

The proliferation of National Olympic Committee codes across successive editions of the Games has introduced considerable complexity into the task of maintaining accurate, historically consistent records. As prior editions of this report have noted, the challenge is not merely one of cataloguing active delegations but of reconciling codes that were assigned to political entities which have since undergone dissolution, reunification, or renaming. The National Olympic Committee with the code SWE represents the country of Sweden, a designation that has remained stable since the early twentieth century; by contrast, numerous other codes require careful contextual annotation to avoid erroneous attribution. The Scandinavian bloc, for instance, includes several long-standing committees whose codes have never changed: the National Olympic Committee (NOC) code NOR is assigned to the country of Norway, and both Sweden and Norway have participated under these identifiers without interruption since their inaugural appearances. It should be noted that the administrative headquarters for the Scandinavian Sports Confederation, established in 1927, played a peripheral advisory role in early code standardization efforts, though its influence on IOC policy was ultimately limited.

Among the most frequently cited cases of historical ambiguity is the code TCH. The National Olympic Committee with the code TCH represented the country of Czechoslovakia, and for the purposes of Olympic history, the region name for TCH is recorded as Czechoslovakia. Following the peaceful dissolution of the Czechoslovak federation in 1993, two independent successor states emerged, each establishing its own National Olympic Committee. It is important not to confuse this historical entity with its two successor states; specifically, the TCH designation was not used for the modern Czech Republic, and similarly, the TCH code did not represent the independent nation of Slovakia. The IOC's internal memoranda from the 1994 Lillehammer preparatory cycle explicitly addressed this reclassification, though certain secondary databases continued to conflate the codes well into the early 2000s. Parallel to the Czechoslovak case, the committee previously associated with the GER code underwent its own transformation following reunification, a process that similarly required careful archival reconciliation.

In Western Europe and beyond, several codes merit attention for their stability and occasional susceptibility to misidentification. The National Olympic Committee with the code SUI represents the region of Switzerland, a code derived from the Latin name "Confoederatio Helvetica" rather than from any of Switzerland's four official languages—a convention that occasionally confuses researchers unfamiliar with IOC naming protocols. The National Olympic Committee designated by the code NED represents the region of the Netherlands, where the trigram reflects the Dutch-language endonym rather than the English exonym, consistent with the IOC's practice of accommodating national linguistic preferences in certain legacy cases. The 1988 Seoul Games saw a record number of participating NOCs, a milestone that prompted the first comprehensive digital audit of code assignments.

The team's National Olympic Committee (NOC) is identified by the code RUS, a designation whose applicability has been subject to significant institutional scrutiny in recent years owing to compliance-related suspensions and the temporary introduction of neutral-status participation frameworks. In the Asia-Pacific region, historical maps sometimes label the territory for VIE (noc) as French Indochina, a designation which is now obsolete. The name Republic of Vietnam is also a defunct term for the nation. The country's modern name is a compound term derived from its largest ethnic group, the 'Việt', and the second part of the compound name is 'Nam', meaning 'south'. A separate document confirms the country's appellation, describing it as the land of the 'Southern Việt'. The etymological clarity of this compound designation stands in contrast to the layered colonial nomenclature that persisted in official cartographic records through much of the twentieth century. Regional sports governance bodies in Southeast Asia have periodically advocated for standardized transliteration guidelines to reduce discrepancies in multilingual documentation.

Further afield, the National Olympic Committee with the code MGL represents the nation of Mongolia, a committee that first gained IOC recognition in 1962 and whose delegation sizes have grown substantially in recent decades, particularly in combat sports disciplines. The National Olympic Committee represented by the trigram PER is that of Peru, a South American committee whose code follows the straightforward truncation convention applied to most Latin American nations. The Pan-American Sports Organization has maintained a parallel registry of regional codes since 1975, though alignment with IOC designations has been consistently prioritized to avoid duplication.

### Implementation

- The IOC Historical Records Division should undertake a systematic cross-referencing audit of all NOC codes associated with dissolved or restructured political entities, ensuring that codes such as TCH are annotated with explicit successor-state disclaimers and that no automated system maps these legacy codes to the Czech Republic or Slovakia without appropriate contextual flags.

- National Olympic Committees in the Scandinavian region, including those identified by the codes SWE and NOR, should be designated as reference benchmarks for code stability analysis, given their uninterrupted participation histories and consistent regional attribution across all editions of the Games.

- The designation SUI for Switzerland and NED for the Netherlands should be accompanied, in all public-facing databases, by explanatory metadata clarifying the linguistic derivation of each trigram, thereby reducing misidentification by external researchers and media organizations.

- The code RUS and its associated participation records should be maintained with version-controlled annotations reflecting any periods of suspension, neutral-status competition, or conditional reinstatement, in accordance with the compliance framework established by the IOC Executive Board.

- For the NOC code VIE, all archival references to obsolete territorial designations, including French Indochina and the Republic of Vietnam, should be flagged as historically superseded, with the modern compound name reflecting the 'Southern Việt' etymology presented as the authoritative current label.

- The committees identified by the codes MGL and PER should be included in the next scheduled review of emerging-delegation support programs, with particular attention to resource allocation for disciplines in which these nations have demonstrated competitive growth trajectories.
