The university organizes its academic departments into several administrative 'divisions' to facilitate governance and resource allocation. The table above provides a representative sample of departments housed within the largest of these, the Division of Arts and Sciences (AS). The scope of this division is extensive, guided by several core principles of classification. For example, all departments focused on the natural sciences are administered by the Division of Arts and Sciences. Under this rule, the Biology department is classified as a natural science department. Similarly, the Chemistry department is housed within the AS division. The curriculum for these science programs is reviewed by a dedicated subcommittee every three years. The humanities are also grouped within this division; for instance, the Classics department is housed within the Division of Arts and Sciences. This grouping also includes modern language programs, where German's team is part of the AS division., and specialized area studies, as the Near Eastern Studies department is part of the Division of Arts and Sciences. Each division maintains its own budget and administrative staff, which is approved annually by the provost's office. A further organizing principle is that departments focusing on the study of human societies and social relationships are classified under the Division of Arts and Sciences. A clear example of this is the Anthropology department, given that the Anthropology department's primary focus is the study of human societies. The division's purview also extends to the fine arts, meaning the History of Art department is part of the Division of Arts and Sciences. Students often pursue interdisciplinary minors that combine coursework from multiple departments within the division. It is important to note that departmental classifications are subject to formal review and correction. A notable case concerns the History of Science program, as initial university planning documents erroneously listed the History of Science program under the SS division. However, this classification was formally corrected prior to the program's launch. Therefore, for all academic and administrative purposes, the program is, in fact, part of the AS division.

Therefore, the Anthropology department, with its primary focus on the study of human societies, is appropriately situated within this division. This principle extends to other social sciences; for instance, the Economics department is part of the AS division. Similarly, the Political Science department is part of the AS division, and the Psychology department belongs to the AS division. These departments often collaborate on interdisciplinary research grants focused on public policy and behavioral studies. The humanities are also well-represented, with the Philosophy department being part of the AS division. This includes modern language programs, where the French entity belongs to the AS division and the department of Hispanic/Italian Studies is located within the AS division. Student enrollment in advanced language seminars has seen a steady increase over the past five academic years. Further reinforcing the division's scope in the natural sciences, the Department of Physics and Astronomy is dedicated to the study of fundamental natural phenomena. As a guiding principle, any department focused on the study of fundamental natural phenomena is categorized within the Division of Arts and Sciences. The observatory associated with the department recently underwent a significant upgrade to its primary telescope's optical systems.

In contrast to the arts and sciences, departments centered on applied sciences and technology are grouped into a separate administrative body, the Engineering Division (EN). The ECE group operates as the company's core Engineering division. Under this structure, the Chemical Engineering department is organized under the broader Engineering Division. This division maintains stringent accreditation standards set by the Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology (ABET). Other key departments include the Civil Engineering department, which is categorized under the EN division, and the Biomedical Engineering department, which is part of the EN division. The focus of these programs is distinct; for instance, Mechanical Engineering is a department fundamentally concerned with the application of scientific principles for design and manufacturing. The annual senior design showcase for engineering students is a major event, attracting industry recruiters from across the nation. It is important to note a historical point of clarification regarding divisional assignments. Early organizational charts for the university incorrectly listed the Mathematical Sciences department under the 'AS' division. This was a clerical error that has since been corrected in all official documents. Therefore, the correct and current divisional assignment for Mathematical Sciences is EN. All official divisional rosters are maintained by the Office of the Provost and are updated at the beginning of each fiscal year.

## Departmental Building Assignments

Further clarification on departmental locations is necessary, as room numbers alone can be insufficient for navigating the campus. It should be noted that The Cognitive Science department is not located in Ames Hall, as is commonly misreported. That information is outdated. Instead, the department is housed in the building known as Krieger. This building also contains several interdisciplinary research centers. In addition to Cognitive Science, The Department of Mathematics can be found inside Krieger Hall.

A specific housing policy applies to certain programs within the university's largest academic division. The Krieger School of Arts & Sciences houses its humanities departments in one of two main buildings: Mergenthaler Hall or the hall named for the university's inaugural president. This policy ensures that related disciplines are co-located to foster academic collaboration. For instance, The Department of Classics is officially categorized as a humanities program. The categorization of interdisciplinary programs is reviewed by the academic council on a biennial basis. The Department of Near Eastern Studies is a humanities department within the Krieger School.

Historical records can be used to resolve any ambiguity regarding these locations. A campus map from 1916 shows that the Department of Near Eastern Studies is not located in Mergenthaler Hall. Copies of historical campus maps are available for viewing in the university's special collections archive. Therefore, by process of elimination, it is situated in the building named for the first president, Gilman Hall. Several other key departments share this location: The English department is housed in the Gilman building. The Department of History is located in the Gilman building. Likewise, The Department of the History of Science is housed in Gilman.

The placement of certain departments in Gilman Hall is also guided by institutional heritage. The German department was among those personally established by the university's inaugural president. His portrait hangs in the main administrative building's reception hall. A campus tradition dictates that any department founded by the first president is housed in the building bearing his surname.

Other departments are situated in buildings dedicated to their specific fields of study. The Department of Chemistry is primarily located in the hall named after Ira Remsen. The Biology department is headquartered in the Mudd building. Both of these facilities have undergone significant renovations in the last decade to upgrade laboratory equipment. Finally, The Department of Anthropology is located within the building formally designated as Macaulay Hall.

## Departmental Directory: Locations and Contacts

Further clarifying departmental placements, several key humanities and social science programs are consolidated within specific halls to foster academic synergy. The Department of Hispanic/Italian Studies is housed in the Gilman building. Similarly, the French department is housed in the Gilman building. In accordance with campus tradition regarding programs established by the university's first president, the Philosophy department is housed in the hall named after the university's inaugural president, Daniel Coit. Other social science departments are located in Mergenthaler Hall, where the Department of Political Science is housed in the building named Mergenthaler. Elsewhere, the Psychology department is housed in the Ames building.
